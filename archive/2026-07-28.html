English insurer Neil Utley acquires historic Château Vignelaure in Provence

  • Neil Utley, English insurance entrepreneur, bought Château Vignelaure on 23 July 2026, a 56-hectare Provence estate founded by Georges Brunet in 1960.
  • Vignelaure pioneered red blends of Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah in northern Coteaux d'Aix; the estate inspired Trévallon and shaped late 20th-century Provence winemaking.
  • The estate has cycled through multiple owners since the 1980s; Utley's purchase marks significant investment in vineyard replanting and cellar renovation, last major updated in 1972.
  • Vignelaure now produces 40% rosé, 20% white, and 40% red; winemaker Philippe Bru will remain in charge and aims to maintain style while improving quality and sales.
  • Utley plans cosmetics and spa ventures using estate grapeseed oil, honey and olives, signalling diversification beyond fine wine as his first wine-industry investment.

Vineyards deploy shade netting to cool grape clusters during heat stress

  • Shade netting is being adopted across regions to lower fruit-zone temperatures and protect anthocyanins, the compounds responsible for red grape color, which degrade above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • At UC Davis's Oakville vineyard, shaded Cabernet Sauvignon clusters showed 27 percent greater anthocyanins than unshaded controls during heat events, with cluster temperatures reaching 58 degrees Celsius without shade.
  • At Petra vineyard in Tuscany, Merlot shaded with nets on the sun-facing side measured 40-41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees in unshaded fruit, reducing sunburn from 40 percent to five percent under 60 percent shade.
  • Net color matters: white and green nets perform better than black nets, which absorb excess heat, and nets must be kept away from clusters to avoid heat transfer from the fabric itself.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses hail netting on 90 percent of vineyards, finding that light netting filters intense altitude sunlight into brief pulses, reducing vine stress without sacrificing beneficial UV exposure.

Mowing cover crops early shields Mediterranean vines from drought stress

  • Researchers at seven organic vineyards in Occitanie, France studied four service crop management methods over 2022-2024 to balance soil health against vine water stress.
  • Early intervention using a mower and tillage before one month after budburst preserved cover crop benefits while eliminating water stress and yield loss versus bare soil.
  • Late interventions and roller-crimper methods reduced yields, berry weight and vigour, showing timing of cover crop management critically shapes vine performance.
  • Results are context-dependent on soil type and microclimate, but statistically significant impacts suggest early mowing offers a climate adaptation tool for drought-prone Mediterranean vineyards.

Grape sunburn spreads beyond hot regions as heat waves intensify

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response to excess light, UV radiation, and berry surface heat; direct sun can raise fruit temperature 22-27°F above air temperature, triggering cell death and flavor damage.
  • Sunburned grapes produce flattened fruit, rubber, asphalt, and creosote notes; winemaker Steve Matthiasson identifies cooked green and fruit flavors, astringent tannins, brown rim, and premature aging in finished wines across multiple regions.
  • Traditional leaf removal at veraison now increases sunburn risk; defoliation at flowering allows berries to build photoprotective compounds, and Chêne Bleu in Ventoux stopped summer leaf removal entirely after 2023 heat damage.
  • Producers deploy kaolin clay film (Tyrrell's in Hunter Valley reports 9°C canopy cooling since 2014), adjust trellis design, retime defoliation, and sort sunburned fruit at harvest or in the winery to prevent flavor damage.

Heat-Tolerant Mourvèdre Struggles as Southern France Temperatures Soar

  • Mourvèdre, bred for heat and drought, is losing balance and elegance in southern France as temperatures and drought intensity exceed even this variety's tolerance.
  • Producers in Bandol and Jumilla are experimenting with canopy management, higher pruning, cover crops, and selective irrigation to help vines survive extreme heat and water stress.
