Seven volcanic regions worldwide rival Mount Etna for complex terroir

  • Volcanic soils from ancient eruptions shape wine in Soave, Italy; Santorini, Greece; the Canary Islands; Hungary's Tokaji region; Oregon's Willamette Valley; Auvergne, France; and California's Lake County.
  • Volcanic terrain creates high-acidity, mineral-driven wines with savory flavors; soils resist pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preservation of old vine varieties.
  • Challenging growing conditions, rugged terrain, intense heat, winds, require adapted viticulture: Santorini trains vines low in basket shapes; Lanzarote hand-digs individual holes called hoyos protected by rock walls.

06Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo bottle conditioning with sugar and yeast for second fermentation, then disgorging and dosage, creating nutty and complex profiles.
  • Ancestral method sparkling wines ferment in bottle without disgorging, remaining fully dry and cloudy with retained lees, predating traditional method techniques.
  • Skin-contact white wines macerate with skins during fermentation, developing color and tannin; the tradition originated in the Caucasus and has resurged in recent decades.
