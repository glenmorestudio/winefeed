Seven US regions seek AVA status to define their wines' distinctive character

  • Seven proposed AVAs in California, Oregon, New Jersey, and New York are under review by the federal Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, a process that takes several years.

  • Deschutes River in central Oregon would be the first recognized winegrowing region there, covering 723,760 acres at 1,500 to 3,500 feet elevation, with growers experimenting alongside traditional varieties on 77 acres.

Costco stocks boutique wines as global producers seek volume sales

  • Costco, the largest US wine retailer, stocks 100-120 rotating wines including boutique producers and own-label Kirkland Signature wines created by contracted winemakers in small lots of around 2000 cases each.

Jameson releases single pot still whiskey inspired by 1826 recipe

  • Jameson, the world's top-selling Irish whiskey, launched Distiller's Batch, a single pot still expression matured in five cask types and bottled at 46% ABV.

03The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·1 OCT 2026

How Can You Train to Recognise Wine Faults?

  • Sietze Wijma founded The Art of Tasting in 2021, teaching wine professionals and enthusiasts to recognize faults using synthesized flavor compounds mimicking real-world defects.
  • Wijma studied psychology at Groningen University, then sensory science at masters level, after discovering aroma compounds could mimic flavors while interning at a UK laboratory.
  • ATHP is the dominant compound causing mousy taint; it becomes volatile only at higher pH and undetectable at normal wine pH, requiring taste to perceive it.

04Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 OCT 2026

Red and White and Roero All Over

  • Roero, north of Alba in Piemonte, grows 1,000 hectares of white Arneis and 360 hectares of red Nebbiolo on sandstone-underlain terrain distinct from the Langhe.
  • Roero Nebbiolo is more approachable than southern neighbors with limber tannins and bright acidity; Arneis is peachy and saline; U.S. prices range $25, $45 for reds and under $25 for whites.
  • Some Roero producers label reds as Langhe Nebbiolo or Nebbiolo d'Alba to exploit laxer production rules and more recognizable names rather than the Roero D.O.C.G. designation.
