Federal 0.4mg THC limit to upend nascent hemp beverage retail market

  • Congress passed legislation in November 2025 capping hemp-derived THC at 0.4 milligrams per container, effective November 12, 2026, effectively banning THC-infused beverages nationwide.
  • The THC-infused beverage market reached $239 million in US retail sales in the 52 weeks prior to April 2026, up 135 percent, with industry experts valuing potential market at $9.9 billion to $14.9 billion.
  • Art Nergaryan, owner of Art's Specialties with four Massachusetts locations, saw sales jump 25 to 35 percent after stocking THC drinks until state regulators banned their sale in wine and liquor stores in May 2024.
  • States have enacted conflicting rules: Minnesota, Georgia, and Tennessee permit hemp beverages while California, Washington, and Vermont restrict or ban them outside licensed cannabis systems.
  • The FDA has ruled interstate commerce in hemp-derived THC beverages unlawful under federal law since 2018, creating a legal gray area that the November 2025 cap aims to resolve.

American single malt ratification fails to lift sales amid broader whiskey downturn

  • The US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau approved a standard of identity for American single malt in December 2024, ending a decade-long push by distillers; hoped-for sales growth has not materialized.
  • Major producers including Westland Distillery, Westward Whiskey, and Balcones Distillery have laid off staff or ceased production as demand fell short of projections in an oversaturated market.
  • Retailers have created dedicated American single malt shelf sections following ratification, and export tracking improved, but consumer awareness remains limited outside connoisseur circles.
  • Distillers including Virginia Distillery Company and Whiskey Del Bac have diversified into lower-priced single malts, other spirits, and blended products to offset sluggish core category sales.
  • Industry leaders attribute slow uptake to difficult economic conditions and bourbon's entrenched position, noting education and distribution remain substantial obstacles for smaller producers.

Seven volcanic regions beyond Etna produce distinctive mineral wines

  • Volcanic soils worldwide create high-acidity, saline, mineral-driven wines with savory rather than fruity character, from Santorini's Assyrtiko to Willamette Valley's Pinot Noir.
  • Santorini, located in the Hellenic Volcanic Arc and due for major eruption within 20 years, preserves vines over 200 years old; basket-trained vines protect against wind and debris in the dry climate.
  • Canary Islands winemakers revived indigenous varieties Listán Blanco and Listán Negro from the 1980s onward, preserved because phylloxera never arrived and volcanic isolation protected native grapes.
  • Volcanic terrain demands unconventional farming: Lanzarote producers hand-dig individual holes called hoyos for vines, each protected by small rock walls against intense heat and wind.
  • Hungary's Tokaji Aszú gains savory, saline edge from volcanic soils; mineral content may protect Furmint, Hárslevelú, and Yellow Muscat grapes from botrytis rot, elevating sweet wines above peers.

Pierce's disease detected in continental Europe; 41 percent of vineyards now at climate risk

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was first detected on mainland European vines in Portugal in 2023; cases now persist in Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards face climate risk from Pierce's disease, double the 21.8 percent estimated by previous models.
  • Vineyard microclimates in river basins and valleys are especially vulnerable because warming winters no longer suppress the bacterium in these topographically complex areas.
  • California growers have managed Pierce's disease since the 1990s through early detection, vector control, and eradication of infected vines; European countries are still developing coordinated surveillance and communication strategies.
  • Eduardo Moralejo, plant pathologist at Institute for Cross-Disciplinary Physics and Complex Systems in Mallorca, warns that silent spread may create false security until the pathogen becomes widely established.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide spraying by up to 80 percent

  • PIWI (fungus-resistant hybrid) grapes require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties, reducing chemical, fuel, and labor costs.
  • Modern genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, used since 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and speeds seedling screening from months to days across thousands of vines.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, four percent of national vineyard area, up 10 percent year-on-year.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental gains versus traditional varieties, chiefly through reduced soil toxicity.
  • French breeders are developing ResDur varieties crossed with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot to maintain regional character while retaining fungal resistance.

Michelin And The Death Of The Wine Guide

  • Michelin's Wine Selection Burgundy 2026 awarded nine producers three grapes, 20 vignerons two grapes, 33 one grape, and 32 were 'selected.'.
  • Domaine Arnoux-Lachaux publicly demanded removal from Michelin's guide after receiving only one grape despite being a celebrated Vosne-Romanée house.
