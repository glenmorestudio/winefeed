Seven restaurant beverage directors reveal wine markup breakdown

  • Seven US beverage directors report wine markups range from 60 to 76 percent of menu price, with actual restaurant profit between 24 and 48 percent per bottle after operational costs.
  • Markup strategy varies by wine: established regions and lower-cost bottles command higher markups, while exclusive or expensive bottles receive modest increases to maintain price-to-quality ratios.
  • Restaurants cite tariffs, labor costs, glassware, insurance, and storage as major factors squeezing margins, forcing tighter pricing as alcohol profit share fell from 60 percent to 30 percent of total hospitality profits.

  • US hemp-beverage retail sales rose 135 percent to $239 million in the year to April 2026, but federal law passed in November 2025 sets a 0.4mg THC limit per container, effective November 12, 2026, which effectively bans the category.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna gain attention for distinctive terroir

  • Volcanic soils create complex, layered terrain that produces high-acidity, mineral-driven wines with savory rather than fruity character.
  • Seven regions, Soave in Italy, Santorini in Greece, the Canary Islands, Tokaji in Hungary, Willamette Valley in Oregon, Auvergne in France, and Lake County in California, share volcanic heritage shaping their signature styles.

Pierce's Disease detected in Europe; 41% of vineyards now at climate risk

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was detected in continental European vineyards for the first time in Portugal in 2023; cases now persist in Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards at climate risk of Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, with river valleys and basins identified as hotspots.
  • California winegrowers, who lost over $48 million annually to Pierce's disease in the 1990s-2000s, are sharing surveillance and vector management strategies with Europe, though European countries still lack unified communication and reporting protocols.

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, then disgorgement and dosage, creating nutty complexity.
  • Ancestral method sparkling wines ferment incompletely in bottle without disgorgement, remaining fully dry and cloudy with retained lees.
  • Tank method (Charmat/Martinotti) uses pressurized tanks for second fermentation, producing clean, direct sparkling wines with minimal lees contact.
