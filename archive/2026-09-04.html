Wildfires destroy 200 hectares in Sierra de Gredos vineyard region

  • Between July 22 and 26, multiple fires destroyed 77,000 hectares across Ávila and Madrid provinces, which contain 85 percent of Sierra de Gredos's 2,000 vineyard hectares.
  • About 200 hectares of vineyards in Cebreros DO, roughly 40 percent of registered vineyard area, suffered fire, smoke, or both damage.
  • Bodegas Bernabeleva lost office space, winery facade, tanks, and equipment; Bodega El Reventón expects 10 to 20 percent vine loss; Comando G's Las Iruelas single-vineyard parcel suffered heavy smoke exposure.
  • Producers including Comando G and Rico Nuevo have submitted proposals to regional authorities requesting firebreak subsidies, vineyard replanting support, and dry-stone wall restoration.
  • Local winemakers argue land abandonment increased fire fuel; they contend viticulture integrated with grazing and forestry management is essential for regional fire prevention.

Federal THC limit threatens US hemp beverage retailers mid-boom

  • THC-infused beverage sales rose 135 percent in 52 weeks to April 2026, reaching $239 million, but face federal ban under November 2025 legislation limiting THC to 0.4 milligrams per container, effective November 12, 2026.
  • Massachusetts retailer Art Nergaryan saw Art's Specialties sales jump 25-35 percent after stocking hemp-derived THC drinks, then plummeted when state regulators banned cannabis beverages from wine and liquor stores in May 2024.
  • Hemp-derived THC exists in a legal gray zone: federally illegal under FDA food law since 2018, but legalized as an agricultural product by the same Farm Bill, leaving states to set conflicting rules.
  • States including Minnesota, Georgia, and Tennessee have legalized hemp beverages, while California, Washington, and Vermont banned them outside licensed cannabis systems, creating a patchwork that blocks national distribution.
  • The Coalition for Adult Beverage Alternatives warns that without federal clarity, retailers, distributors, and manufacturers face constant regulatory reversals; the upcoming federal THC cap will force compliance or market exit.

American single malt ratification fails to lift sales as distillers struggle

  • The US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau approved American single malt as an official whiskey category in December 2024 after a decade of petitioning by distillers, but sales have not improved as hoped.
  • Westland Distillery in Seattle lost $1 million to theft and laid off staff; Diageo-owned Balcones in Waco stopped production in August 2025 and cut 17 jobs; Westward Whiskey in Portland filed for bankruptcy after its parent Distill Ventures wound down.
  • The official standard has helped retailers create dedicated shelf space and improved export tracking, but most consumers remain unaware of American single malt or confuse it with bourbon and Scotch.
  • Small and mid-sized producers face oversaturated retail shelves and consolidated distribution networks; Virginia Distillery saw single malt sales fall from two-thirds to one-third of revenue as consumers prefer lower-priced cask-finished variants.
  • Distillers are adapting by launching lower-priced products, blends with bourbon, and diversifying into rye, tequila and vodka seltzer rather than waiting for category-wide demand to grow.

Early harvests shift wine tourism and challenge logistics across regions

  • Domaine Lafage in Roussillon began picking grapes July 23, weeks earlier than historical norms; California Central Coast harvests started six to eight weeks ahead of traditional schedules.
  • Aaron Jackson, winemaker at Aaron Wines in Paso Robles, reports July visitation drops sharply as temperatures rise; heat deters visitors and makes shipping full-bodied reds unsafe during summer.
  • Šárka Betke, co-founder of Lacery vineyard in Moravia, Czech Republic, saw summer wine tourism decline and shipping delays that pushed revenue collection into fall.
  • Wine-region travel is shifting later into fall as warming temperatures improve October-November conditions; Mark Hallett at Grape Escapes reports Champagne bookings surge for November.
  • Earlier harvests strain logistics: refrigeration costs spike when peak cooling demand hits August instead of October; bottling crews, custom crush facilities, and glass suppliers face compressed timelines.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna offer complex terroir and distinctive styles

  • Volcanic soils from ancient eruptions create complex, layered earth that produces wines marked by high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors rather than fruit-forward profiles.
  • Soave in Italy, dormant for 25 million years, still expresses volcanic terroir through Garganega wines with orchard fruit and mineral-driven acidity.
  • Santorini, in one of the world's most volcanically active zones, grows 200-year-old Assyrtiko vines trained low in basket shapes to protect against wind and debris; the wine shows mineral depth unmatched elsewhere in Greece.
  • The Canary Islands' isolation from volcanic closures and phylloxera allowed old-vine varieties like Listán Blanco and Listán Negro to survive; Lanzarote producers hand-dig individual vine holes called hoyos to combat heat and wind.

Pierce's Disease Detected in Continental Europe; 41% of Vineyards at Climate Risk

  • Pierce's disease, caused by Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, was first detected on continental European grapevines in Portugal in 2023 after infecting olives and Spanish vineyards since 2013.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now at climate risk, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, with river valleys especially vulnerable.
  • California's decades-long battle with Pierce's disease offers Europe lessons in early detection and vector management, though infected vines typically require destruction once established.
  • European countries lack unified reporting mandates and communication strategies, risking concealment of infections; California's collaborative culture and early self-reporting slowed but did not stop spread.
  • Warming winters no longer suppress the bacterium's survival, allowing silent expansion through complex topography before visible epidemics emerge.

Disease-resistant hybrids cut fungicide sprays 80 percent, gain European vineyard foothold

  • PIWIs (fungus-resistant hybrid grapes) require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional Vitis vinifera varieties, cutting chemical use by up to 80 percent.
  • Modern backcrossing techniques now produce PIWIs containing 95 percent vinifera genetic material, preserving wine quality while retaining disease resistance from American vine species.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, identifies precise locations of resistance genes in 2,000 genetic markers, accelerating breeding precision.
  • Germany leads PIWI plantings at 4,000 hectares in 2025, up 10 percent annually; France is developing ResDur varieties crossed with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot for regional expression.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental benefits versus traditional varieties, mainly through reduced spraying costs and improved soil ecology.

Remembering Branko Čotar

  • Branko Čotar, pioneering natural winemaker from Slovenia's Karst region, died aged 78 on 27 August following a short illness.
  • Čotar started with 500 vines for his restaurant in 1974, began commercial bottling in 1990, and built a winery producing around 30,000 bottles annually.
  • He pioneered orange wines with no added sulphites in the 1980s, two decades before Radikon and Gravner went public with the concept in the late 1990s.
  • Čotar signed with US importer Louis Dressner in 2003 and became one of the first members of Luca Gargano's Triple A portfolio that year.
  • Grapes were grown organically with traditional skin-fermentation of white grapes for around a week; no additives, corrections, or sulphites used during vinification.

03Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, creating nutty and complex wines through bottle conditioning and aging.
  • Ancestral method sparkling wines ferment in bottle before reaching dryness, retain lees, and remain fully dry and savory without disgorgement.
  • Tank-method sparkling wines ferment in pressurized tanks with added sugar and yeast, producing clean and direct styles with minimal lees contact.
  • Skin-contact wines macerate white grapes with skins during fermentation, developing color, tannin, and flavor; the tradition originated in the Caucasus and has resurged in the last two decades.
  • Wine method, the production technique, determines finished style: traditional, ancestral, and tank methods always produce sparkling wines; passito always produces sweet wines; sur lie always produces rounded and savory wines.
