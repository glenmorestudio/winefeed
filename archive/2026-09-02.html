Seven sommeliers reveal wine markup ranges from 60 to 76 percent of bill

  • Seven beverage directors across US restaurants reported wine markups ranging from 60 to 76 percent of the final menu price, depending on venue and strategy.
  • Kinga Mackowiak at Apple Blossom in Denver reported 60 to 70 percent markup, while William Jones at Deep Roots Restaurant Group in Alabama averaged 76 percent markup across multiple locations.
  • Markups fund invisible costs: glassware, labor, insurance, credit card fees, storage, and utilities; restaurants also invest in wine selection, proper service, and temperature control.
  • Rising tariffs, labor costs, and operational expenses have compressed profit margins; Deep Roots and Renzo in Charleston adjusted pricing strategies to maintain target profitability despite external pressures.
  • Beverage directors price dynamically rather than applying flat multipliers, charging higher markups on lower-priced bottles and lower markups on expensive bottles to balance accessibility and revenue.

Spain's devastating Gredos wildfires damage emerging fine wine region

  • Record Spanish wildfires burned 77,000 hectares across Ávila and Madrid provinces between July 22-26, destroying roughly 40 percent of Cebreros DO's 500 registered vineyard hectares.
  • Bodegas Bernabeleva lost its office, facade, windows, and equipment; Bodega El Reventón's San Gregorio parcel burned, though owners expect to recover 80-90 percent of vines.
  • Comando G's Las Iruelas single-vineyard wine in Cebreros suffered heavy smoke exposure and will likely not be produced this vintage, though their main vineyard sites in Villanueva and Cadalso de los Vidrios were spared.
  • Local producers including Comando G founders Daniel Landi and Fernando García have submitted fire-prevention proposals to regional authorities requesting subsidies for forest clearance, firebreaks, and vineyard replanting.
  • Juanan Martín of Rico Nuevo argues fire risk reflects decades of declining rural management and livestock farming, not climate change alone, positioning vineyard expansion as essential land-use infrastructure for Gredos.

Federal THC limit derails booming hemp beverage retail market

  • THC-infused beverage sales surged 135 percent to $239 million in the year to April 2026, then crashed when Massachusetts banned them from wine stores in May 2024.
  • Art Nergaryan, owner of Art's Specialties wine shops in Massachusetts, saw revenue jump 25 to 35 percent after stocking hemp drinks, then lost the category overnight due to state regulatory guidance.
  • Congress passed legislation in November 2025 capping total THC at 0.3 milligrams per beverage container, effective November 12, 2026, which effectively bans most hemp-derived THC drinks nationwide.
  • States have enacted conflicting rules: Minnesota and Georgia permit hemp beverages while California, Washington, and Vermont restrict or ban them, creating an impossible patchwork for national distribution.
  • The FDA declared interstate THC-beverage commerce unlawful in 2018; the industry has operated in legal gray area despite growing demand and potential $9.9 billion to $14.9 billion market value.

American single malt ratification fails to lift sales as distilleries struggle

  • The US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau approved American single malt as an official whiskey category in December 2024 after a decade of petitioning by distillers.
  • Major producers including Westland Distillery in Seattle, Balcones in Waco Texas, and Westward Whiskey in Portland have closed operations or cut staff as sales remain flat despite ratification.
  • Retailers have created dedicated shelf space for American single malt following the standard, and consumers no longer conflate it with peated Scotch, but broader market adoption has stalled.
  • Distillers report that bourbon dominates American whiskey perception and that retail shelf consolidation limits distribution opportunities for smaller producers regardless of category recognition.
  • Many single malt producers have diversified into cheaper cask-finished variants, bourbon blends, and other spirits to offset slow growth in the core American single malt category.

World Cup failed to halt US beer industry's sales decline

  • Anheuser-Busch InBev invested $110 million in World Cup marketing but reported only 0.25% volume lift, while US beer sales fell 4.5% year-to-date through mid-2026.
  • Off-premise beer sales declined 2.1% in dollars and 3.4% in volume for the week ending July 19, the tournament's final day, worse than prior four-week and year-to-date trends.
  • On-premise draft beer sales rose 7.1% in dollars and 6.46% in volume during the tournament, but gains evaporated by the final week, ending at -1% and -3% respectively versus 2025.
  • Spirits outperformed beer during the same period, with category sales up 4.4% in dollars and 8.4% in volume, driven by ready-to-drink cocktail brands like Cutwater and Surfside.
  • Industry shed approximately 3.5 million barrels in the first half of 2026 versus 2025, suggesting the World Cup's once-every-four-years occasion could not overcome structural beer market weakness.

Seven volcanic regions beyond Etna showcase distinctive mineral wines

  • Volcanic soils from past eruptions create wines with high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors distinct from fruit-forward styles elsewhere.

  • Producers including Gaia Wines in Santorini, Royal Tokaji in Hungary, and Evening Land in Oregon exemplify winemakers revitalizing volcanic regions through quality-focused viticulture.

Pierce's disease bacteria detected in continental Europe; 41% of vineyards at climate risk

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was first detected on continental European grapevines in Portugal in 2023, one infected vine that was managed swiftly.
  • A 2025 climate study found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face climate risk from Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in earlier models, driven by warming winters.
  • Vineyard microclimates near river basins and valleys are especially vulnerable because topography allows the pathogen to spread between areas that historically remained isolated by cold.
  • California's three-decade battle with Pierce's disease, which cost growers over $48 million annually, offers Europe lessons in early detection and vector management, though strategies must fit local conditions.
  • European countries lack unified reporting and communication protocols; California's voluntary early disclosure, driven by culture and economic incentive, contrasts with Europe's eradication-only mandate that risks concealment.

Disease-resistant hybrids cut fungicide use 80 percent, gaining ground in Europe

  • PIWIs, fungus-resistant hybrid grapes bred from European vinifera and American species, reduce fungicide sprays from eight to 15 times yearly to two to three times at Famille Ducourt in Bordeaux.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by US researchers in 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and speeds breeding; marker-assisted selection now scans 2,000 genetic markers across 4,000 seedlings in days instead of months.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption at 4,000 hectares in 2025, four percent of total vineyard area, with 10 percent annual growth; Luxembourg data show PIWIs deliver twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit reduction versus traditional varieties.
  • French breeders develop ResDur varieties by crossing with regional grapes like Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot to retain Bordeaux signature; Bordeaux University will plant experimental hybrid plots over two years.
  • Acceptance depends on consumer taste validation; direct-to-consumer sales dominate at 71 percent in Germany, with retail expanding as supermarket chains begin stocking PIWIs.

02Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, then are disgorged and topped with dosage; brut nature receives no added sugar.
  • Ancestral method sparkling wine ferments in bottle before reaching dryness, retains lees, and is typically not disgorged, creating a dry, lightly cloudy, savory style.
  • Tank method (Charmat/Martinotti) uses pressurized tanks for second fermentation, producing clean, direct sparkling wines with minimal lees contact.
  • Skin-contact white wines macerate with skins, seeds, and pulp during fermentation, developing color, tannin, and complex flavors; the tradition originated in the Caucasus.
  • Wine method dictates finished style: traditional/ancestral/tank methods always produce sparkling; passito always produces sweet wine; sous voile creates nutty, oxidative wines.
