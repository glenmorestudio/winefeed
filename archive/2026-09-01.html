Record Spanish wildfires damage Sierra de Gredos vineyards, threatening region's revival

  • Between July 22 and 26, wildfires destroyed 77,000 hectares across Ávila and Madrid provinces, which contain 85 percent of Sierra de Gredos's 2,000 vineyard hectares.
  • Approximately 200 hectares of vineyards in Cebreros DO, roughly 40 percent of registered area, suffered fire, smoke, or both damage.
  • Bodegas Bernabeleva lost office space, facade sections, windows, tanks, and equipment; Bodega El Reventón lost the San Gregorio parcel but expects 80 to 90 percent vine recovery.
  • Comando G's Las Iruelas single-vineyard wine, the region's flagship, likely lost to heavy smoke exposure, though their primary vineyard sites in Villanueva, Navatalgordo, and Cadalso de los Vidrios escaped fire.
  • Wine producers have proposed regional subsidies for forest clearance and firebreaks to prevent future disasters, framing vineyard maintenance as rural fire prevention infrastructure.

Federal THC drink ban upends state retail markets as rules shift overnight

  • Massachusetts retailers selling hemp-derived THC beverages faced a May 2024 state ban on these products in wine and liquor stores, forcing distributors to pull inventory overnight and crushing sales.
  • Art Nergaryan, owner of Art's Specialties with four Massachusetts locations, saw revenue jump 25 to 35 percent after stocking THC drinks, then plummet when the ban took effect with no warning.
  • Federal law will impose a 0.4 milligram THC per container limit effective November 12, 2026, with enforcement by December 11, 2026, effectively banning most hemp-derived THC beverages nationwide.
  • The THC-infused beverage market grew 135 percent to $239 million in 52 weeks prior to April 2026, but federal and state rules now conflict, leaving retailers, distributors, and manufacturers navigating an illegal gray area.
  • States regulate hemp drinks inconsistently, Minnesota and Georgia permit them, while California, Washington, and Vermont ban them outside licensed cannabis systems, making compliant nationwide distribution nearly impossible.

English estates launch prestige cuvées to rival Champagne's super-premium tier

American single malt ratification fails to boost sales amid whiskey downturn

  • The Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau approved a standard of identity for American single malt in December 2024, the first new whiskey category in over 50 years, after a decade of petitioning by the American Single Malt Whiskey Commission.
  • The category faces a crowded retail shelf and weak consumer recognition outside connoisseur circles, even as Jack Daniel's and other major bourbon brands released American single malt lines.
  • Retailers have created dedicated shelf sections for American single malt in response to the official designation, and consumers are now distinguishing it from peated Scotch and bourbon mash bills.
  • Producers are adapting by diversifying into other spirits: Virginia Distillery Company shifted sales to cheaper cask-finished single malts and launched tequila and vodka brands; Whiskey Del Bac added bourbon and rye to offset single malt weakness.

Burgenland emerges as Austria's quality wine powerhouse after EU investment

  • Burgenland, ceded to Austria in 1920 and isolated by the Iron Curtain until 1995, received over €1 billion in EU Objective 1 investment funding that rebuilt its wine infrastructure and economy.
  • Judith Beck's reinvention of Welschriesling as a dry skin-contact wine exemplifies how growers reclaimed forgotten varieties and vineyards to suit contemporary taste rather than Austria's traditional paradigm of bulk reds and easy whites.
  • Burgenland's relatively inexpensive vineyards, 26 percent organic acreage, and proximity to Vienna's natural wine bars enabled early adoption of low-intervention winemaking; Gut Oggau became a sensation at the 2014 RAW wine fair in Vienna.
  • Producer collectives like Pannobile, founded 30 years ago by Hans Nittnaus around Gols, fostered peer criticism and experimentation that propelled members including Beck, Preisinger, and Heinrich to international recognition.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Etna showcase terroir shaped by ancient eruptions

  • Volcanic soils create complex, layered terrain from lava flows and debris; they are naturally hostile to pests and disease, making organic farming easier than in non-volcanic regions.
  • Soave in Italy, dormant for 25 million years, produces high-acidity whites from Garganega grapes that express the region's ancient volcanic substratum.
  • Santorini, in the Hellenic Volcanic Arc, grows 200-year-old Assyrtiko vines in basket-trained rows that protect against wind and debris; the island's mineral-driven whites are unmatched elsewhere in Greece.
  • The Canary Islands' volcanic isolation prevented phylloxera and foreign influence, preserving old-vine varieties like Listán Blanco and Listán Negro; Lanzarote's intense heat requires hand-dug individual vine holes called hoyos.
  • Oregon's Willamette Valley volcanic Jory soil produces red-fruit Pinot Noirs in Dundee Hills and Chehalem Mountains, contrasting marine sediment's darker fruit expression in the same region.

Pierce's disease detected in Europe; warming climate threatens 41% of vineyards

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease by blocking water transport in grapevines, was found in continental European grapevines for the first time in Portugal in 2023, then identified in Spain and France.
  • A 2025 climate study using high-resolution data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face climate risk from Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in earlier models, with river valleys especially vulnerable.
  • California growers and researchers are sharing decades of experience with Pierce's disease management, including early detection, vector control, and genomic surveillance, to help European regions prepare before the pathogen becomes endemic.
  • European countries lack unified communication and reporting strategies; some have no mandate to report infections or destroy vines, creating risk of concealment and uncontrolled spread unlike California's culture of early disclosure.
  • Winemakers in affected regions including Mallorca and Sicily are adopting long-term coexistence strategies focused on vineyard health and early detection rather than fear-driven responses to inevitable vine losses.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent in European vineyards

  • PIWIs, fungus-resistant hybrid varieties bred by crossing non-vinifera American vines with European vinifera, reduce fungicide applications from 8-15 sprays yearly to 2-3 for powdery and downy mildew control.
  • German grape breeders developed modern PIWIs in the late 20th century; today's varieties contain up to 95 percent vinifera genetic material through backcrossing, preserving traditional wine quality.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019 under the US VitisGen initiative, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and enables marker-assisted selection of seedlings in days rather than months.
  • Germany leads adoption with 4,000 hectares of PIWI vines in 2025, representing 10 percent growth; Bordeaux University and French researchers are developing ResDur varieties that retain regional character while resisting mildew.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit versus traditional varieties, with gains in soil ecology and reduced water toxicity offsetting higher vine costs.
