Younger Bordeaux vintages surge at iDealwine as collectors shift to drink-now wines

  • iDealwine, an online auction house with offices in Paris, Hong Kong, Singapore and New York, reported record trading in 2025: 391,000 bottles worth €33.9 million, up 9% in value and 19% in volume.
  • Bordeaux volumes surged 23% to 105,000+ bottles despite widespread industry talk of oversupply; value sales rose 19% to €12.5 million while prices held nearly flat, outperforming the broader market's 8% decline.
  • Bordeaux wines under 10 years old jumped to 28% of auction lots in 2025 from 20% the year before, driven by modern styles that are drinkable young rather than requiring decades of cellaring.
  • The 2025 Bordeaux en primeur campaign saw weak overall demand despite attractive pricing, suggesting fewer collectors want to buy-and-hold; most buyers now prefer wines to open the same night.
  • Château Lafleur volumes more than doubled at lower prices (average €471, down 25%), possibly linked to its withdrawal from the Pomerol appellation; Château Figeac volumes rose 72% with stable pricing after its 2022 elevation to premier grand cru classé A.

Australian wine exports fall to 22-year low amid global consumption collapse

  • Wine Australia reported exports fell 7% in value to A$2.3bn and 6% in volume to 598 million litres in the year to June 2026, the lowest volume since 2004.
  • Global wine consumption has fallen to its lowest level since 1961, driven by cost-of-living pressures, changing consumer habits and competition from alternative alcoholic beverages.
  • Australia's three largest markets all declined: mainland China dropped 15% to A$756m despite remaining the top market, while UK and US shipments hit 25-year lows.
  • Weakness in the UK focused on lower and mid-priced wines, while US demand softened across most price categories due to lower retailer inventories and domestic competition.
  • Canada surged 20% in value to A$188m, aided by market-share gains from reduced US wine availability during trade disputes, while several Asian markets outside China also grew.

Vineyards deploy shade netting to cool grape clusters during heat stress

  • Shade netting is being tested across California, Argentina, Italy, and Piedmont to lower cluster temperatures and protect color and acidity during heat spikes.
  • A 2022 UC Davis trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded controls when temperatures exceeded 35°C degradation threshold.
  • At Petra estate in Tuscany, Merlot clusters under 90 percent shade nets measured 40-41°C versus 50°C unshaded; sunburn fell from 41 percent to 3 percent in one trial.
  • Net design matters: white and green nets perform better than black, and nets must be positioned away from fruit to avoid heat-trapping contact; more shade does not always mean cooler berries.
  • Boroli in Barolo's Villero vineyard found an open net reduced maximum temperatures from 49.2°C to 43°C, but darker nets exceeded 50°C, demonstrating netting's complexity as a heat-adaptation tool.

Grape sunburn spreads as heat waves reach cool regions, forcing vineyard rethink

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response to excess light and ultraviolet radiation, but primarily to berry surface temperature; direct sun can push fruit 22-27°F above air temperature, causing cells to die, skins to crack, and berries to shrivel.
  • Joanna Gambetta, Ph.D., wine scientist at University of Newcastle Australia, found that Chardonnay berries show damage within five minutes at surface temperatures of 104-109°F under high light; browning appears at lower heat, necrosis at extreme temperatures.
  • Sunburned grapes taste of cooked and oxidized fruit, fruit leather, rubber, asphalt, and creosote; in finished wine they show flabby acid, high alcohol, light color with brown rim, astringent tannins, and premature aging that worsens over time.
  • As climate change pushes heat waves into historically cool regions, vineyard management practices that worked in moderate summers are being retimed or abandoned; sorting sunburned fruit at harvest is now critical defense.

Heat-Tolerant Mourvèdre Struggles as Southern France Temperatures Soar

  • Mourvèdre, long bred for heat in Bandol and Jumilla, now faces extreme temperatures and drought that push even this variety toward imbalance and vine death.
  • A three-year drought in Jumilla from 2022 to 2024 killed old bush vines; surface area there halved in 20 years as producers abandoned hotter southern sites.
  • Bandol producers including Jean-François Ott at Domaines Ott and the Gueissards are adapting canopy management, higher pruning, and ground cover to shade vines and retain soil moisture.
  • Irrigation, long rejected in dry-farmed Provence, is now deemed essential by viticulture professor Alain Deloire and accepted by some Bandol growers to prevent vine death in severe years.
  • Wine style is shifting: cooler Bandol sites traditionally used for rosé are now reserved for red wine; rosé production may decline as the region prioritizes survival of remaining vineyards.
