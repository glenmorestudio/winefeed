Vineyard idling gains traction as producers weather weak grape demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary pause in production lasting one to three years, allows producers to preserve assets and capital while avoiding permanent removal decisions during uncertain market conditions.
  • Viticulturists managing idled vineyards must maintain strict irrigation, aggressive pruning, weed control, and minimal fungicide protocols to prevent long-term vine damage and enable quick return to production.

Seven volcanic wine regions rival Etna for mineral complexity and terroir

  • Santorini, Soave, the Canary Islands, Hungary's Tokaji region, Oregon's Willamette Valley, France's Auvergne, and California's Lake County all produce distinctive wines from ancient or dormant volcanic soils.
  • Volcanic terroir yields high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors; soils hostile to pests enable organic farming and preserve old grape varieties including Assyrtiko, Garganega, Listán Blanco, Furmint, and Gamay d'Auvergne.
  • Challenging growing conditions, Santorini's basket-trained vines, Lanzarote's hand-dug hoyos, Willamette's Jory soil, shape viticulture methods and the red or mineral fruit profiles of the resulting wines.

Myths of The Douro

  • The Douro now produces as much table wine as port, but global port sales have declined 25% over the last two decades.
