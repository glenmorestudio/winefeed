Texas High Plains grape growers face market pressure as local demand softens

  • Texas wineries saw sales surge during the pandemic, then inventories backed up as consumer demand softened from 2023 onward, leaving growers with unsold fruit.
  • Akhil Reddy of Reddy Vineyards (162 hectares, 38 varieties near Brownfield) and Katy Jane Seaton of Farmhouse Vineyards report wineries now avoid multi-year contracts, wait for harvest spot-market deals, and buy only contracted fruit.
  • Growers respond by cutting care on uncontracted blocks to bare maintenance; fully contracted acreage receives premium farming, creating two-tier economics on the same property.
  • Connor Wilmeth, vineyard manager at Lahey Vineyards (405 hectares), is now selling Texas grapes to East Coast buyers to offset local softness and leverage shorter freight routes versus California.
  • Veteran growers including Jet Wilmeth and Andy Timmons are reducing acreage or retiring; Neal Newsom (benchmark grower for decades) seeks a working partner to sustain his vineyard through succession.

Light exposure drives pyrazine levels in wine grapes, research shows

  • Methoxypyrazines, or pyrazines, are nitrogen-based aroma compounds that create green, herbal, and peppery notes in wine, particularly in Cabernet Franc, Sauvignon Blanc, and related varieties.
  • Light exposure is the primary factor controlling pyrazine concentration in grapes; shoot and leaf removal increases light on clusters and reduces pyrazine levels, as Dominique Roujou de Boubee's 1995 University of Bordeaux research demonstrated.
  • Vine rootstock choice affects pyrazines indirectly through the vigor and canopy density it produces; eight different Cabernet Sauvignon rootstocks showed significant variation in grape and stem pyrazine content.
  • Soil type influences pyrazines via water retention and vigour: well-drained gravel soils produce lower pyrazine grapes than clay-silt or limestone soils in Bordeaux conditions.
  • Temperature's role remains unclear despite winemakers observing fewer pyrazines in warmer sites; light and heat are difficult to separate in vineyard studies.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide spraying by 80 percent across Europe

  • PIWIs, hybrid grapes bred for mildew resistance, require two to three fungicide sprays yearly versus eight to 15 for traditional vinifera varieties, cutting chemical use by up to 80 percent.
  • Modern genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and allows breeders to stack multiple resistance layers into single varieties.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, representing four percent of German vineyards; France is developing ResDur hybrids retaining regional signatures like Bordeaux character.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefits versus traditional varieties, with largest gains in soil ecology and water quality.
  • Industry tastings with mature PIWI wines from established vineyards prove critical to acceptance; growers report PIWIs require no vineyard practice changes beyond reduced spraying.

Vineyards test netting to cool grape clusters during heat events

  • Vineyard netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is being deployed to lower cluster temperature and preserve color, acidity, and phenolics during heat spikes.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit during heat stress, as anthocyanins degrade above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses hail netting on 90 percent of vineyards to filter direct sunlight into brief pulses rather than prolonged exposure, preventing vine stress in high-altitude desert conditions.
  • Petra estate in Tuscany found netting reduced Merlot cluster temperature from 50 degrees to 40-41 degrees Celsius and cut sunburn incidence from 41 percent to 3 percent under 90 percent shade.
  • Net color matters: white and green nets perform better than black; placement must avoid contact with clusters to prevent heat transfer, and tradeoffs include altered airflow, disease pressure, and phenolic development.

03The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·12 AUG 2026

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU law limits volatile acidity in wine to 1.2 g/L for reds, 1.08 g/L for whites; Sébastien David's Loire Cabernet Franc and Pittis' orange wine Mash Pitt exceeded these limits and faced destruction orders.
  • The US permits 1.4 g/L for red wines, rising to 1.7 g/L for super-ripe grapes; the EU's updated 2025 exceptions list allows up to 2.4 g/L for hundreds of appellations including Amarone, Madeira, and Tokaji.
  • Volatile acidity becomes unsafe for consumption only at 10% concentration; the EU's 1.2 g/L limit sits between safe wine levels and vinegar's 40 g/L minimum, with no documented health risk justifying the regulation.
  • Orange wines, which are skin-fermented like reds, must comply with the lower white wine VA limit of 1.08 g/L despite having tannin profiles similar to red wines.

  • Drinking studies is an interdisciplinary field treating alcohol as cultural and social practice, not merely a drink or health hazard.
