Seven sommeliers reveal wine markups span 60 to 76 percent of restaurant bill

  • Seven US beverage directors shared how they calculate wine markups, ranging from 60 to 76 percent of the final menu price across Denver, Alabama, Charleston, Boston, and Phoenix venues.
  • Kinga Mackowiak at Apple Blossom in Denver prices wines with 60 to 70 percent markup but keeps only 38 to 48 percent as profit after glassware, labor, and credit card fees.
  • William Jones at Deep Roots Restaurant Group in Alabama targets 58 percent prime cost including labor and insurance, yielding 42 percent revenue per bottle, but tariffs and wage rises now threaten those goals.
  • Beverage directors price strategically by wine type, demand, and inventory age rather than applying a single multiplier; established regions support higher markups while cheaper bottles carry larger percentage increases.
  • Alcohol sales, once 60 percent of restaurant profits, now hover near 30 percent as fewer consumers dine out, forcing operators to defend markups by emphasizing selection, storage, service, and proper glassware.

Early harvests shift wine tourism and logistics across global regions

  • Wine tourism is shifting: Aaron Jackson, winemaker at Aaron Wines in Paso Robles, California, reports July visitation drops sharply as heat deters tasting-room visits, and hot weather discourages buyers from transporting wine home.
  • Operating costs are rising across production: refrigeration and electricity peak during August instead of October when temperatures are higher; insurance and water fees are climbing; label application fails in excessive heat, requiring costly rework.
  • Tourism upside offsets some losses: earlier fall weather in regions like Champagne and Burgundy is extending wine tourism into November, and harvest season now overlaps peak summer travel, potentially boosting visitor numbers to wine regions.

Spain's devastating wildfires ravage Sierra de Gredos wine revival

  • Record Spanish wildfires burned 77,000 hectares across Ávila and Madrid provinces between July 22-26, destroying approximately 200 hectares of vineyards in Cebreros DO, roughly 40 percent of the region's registered vineyard area.
  • Bodegas Bernabeleva in San Martín de Valdeiglesias lost its office, winery facade, windows, and equipment; Bodega El Reventón's San Gregorio vineyard was severely damaged, though owners expect to recover 80-90 percent of vines.
  • Comando G's Las Iruelas single-vineyard bottling in Cebreros suffered heavy smoke exposure and will likely not be produced this vintage, though the founders' main vineyard sites in other locations were spared.
  • Daniel Landi and Fernando García of Comando G have co-drafted proposals to regional authorities requesting forest clearance subsidies, firebreak creation, and support for vineyard planting and dry-stone wall restoration.
  • Local producers argue fire prevention must integrate rural development, livestock farming, and agricultural management; vineyards act as a mosaic element that maintains active land use and reduces fuel accumulation in the countryside.

American single malt ratification fails to lift sales as category struggles

  • The US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau ratified American single malt as an official whiskey category in December 2024 after a decade of industry petitioning, but anticipated sales gains have not materialized.
  • Westland Distillery in Seattle lost $1 million to theft in 2025 and laid off staff; Diageo shut Balcones in Waco, Texas, laying off 17 workers in August 2025; Westward Whiskey in Portland filed for bankruptcy when its parent Distill Ventures wound down.
  • The official standard has helped retailers create dedicated shelf sections and improved export tracking, but most consumers outside whiskey connoisseur circles remain unaware the category exists.
  • Distillers report bourbon's dominance and retail shelf oversaturation make growth difficult; Virginia Distillery Company's single malt sales fell from two-thirds to one-third of revenue in two years as consumers gravitated to cheaper cask-finished versions.
  • Producers are adapting by diversifying into other spirits, blending single malt with bourbon as a gateway product, and expanding rye and bourbon lines to survive current market conditions.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Etna merit exploration for distinctive terroir

  • Volcanic soils worldwide create distinctive high-acidity, mineral-driven wines with savory rather than fruity profiles, drawing wine interest beyond Mount Etna.

  • Auvergne in central France and Lake County in California round out volcanic regions where Gamay d'Auvergne and Cabernet Sauvignon express distinctive mineral and tannin character.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41 Percent of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • Pierce's disease, caused by the bacterium Xylella fastidiosa, has infected grapevines in Portugal, Spain, Italy, and France; it killed large vineyard blocks in California starting in the 1990s, costing growers over $48 million annually.
  • A 2025 high-resolution climate study found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face climate risk of Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in earlier models, driven by warming winters and vulnerable vineyard microclimates near river basins.
  • The bacterium thrives in warm temperatures; historically cold European winters suppressed it, but warming is erasing that natural barrier in topographically complex areas that may become disease hotspots.
  • European growers and agronomists fear vine destruction mandates; unlike California's voluntary reporting system, EU regulations lack coordination between regions, risking concealment of infections and delayed detection.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent in European vineyards

  • PIWIs, fungus-resistant hybrid grapes bred from American and European vines, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties like Cabernet Franc.
  • Genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed globally since 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision, allowing breeders to stack multiple layers of protection into single varieties.
  • Germany leads adoption with 4,000 hectares of PIWI vines in 2025, up ten percent year-on-year, while France develops ResDur varieties designed to retain regional signatures in Bordeaux and elsewhere.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental benefit versus traditional varieties, mainly through reduced spraying costs and lower soil and water toxicity.
  • Direct-to-consumer sales dominate PIWI markets at 71 percent in Germany; retail expansion depends on major supermarkets stocking them and growers translating vineyard savings into competitive pricing.

03Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, then are disgorged and dosed; they develop nutty, complex flavors from bottle conditioning.
  • Ancestral method sparkling wines ferment in bottle without disgorgement, remain fully dry and lightly cloudy with retained lees, predating traditional method winemaking.
  • Tank method (Charmat/Martinotti) uses pressurized tanks for second fermentation, producing clean, direct sparkling wines with minimal lees contact.
  • Skin-contact wines macerate white grapes with skins during fermentation, producing gold to deep orange color and increased tannin; the tradition originated in the Caucasus.
  • Botrytis cinerea fungus concentrates grape sugars by shriveling berries, creating honeyed and lightly savory white wines called noble rot or pourriture noble.
