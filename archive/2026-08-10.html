Anheuser-Busch becomes top wine marketer via BeatBox acquisition

  • African Development Bank projects Africa's middle class will exceed 500 million by 2030, with urban population doubling to 1.4 billion by 2050, generating forecast wine market of 1.8 billion litres and $8 billion by 2035.

Ancient vine-training systems decline as labor costs and mechanization reshape European viticulture

  • Alberata vines in Italy, hoyos in Lanzarote, bellussera in Veneto, and other historical training methods face extinction due to high manual labor costs and pressure to mechanize.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo vineyards lost 437 hectares between 2000 and 2026 as growers abandoned labor-intensive pits, despite their role in the island's tourism economy.
  • Tourism and wine quality support some systems: Lanzarote introduced subsidies recognizing hoyos' economic value; Familia Torres uses adapted machinery to maintain Rías Baixas pergolas; climate adaptation may revive bellussera.
  • Loss of these systems erodes rural heritage, local know-how, and viticultural diversity, traits now vital to climate resilience, while reshaping landscapes that once drew visitors and defined regions.
  • Survival depends on region-specific adaptation: flat terrain and vanished industries doomed bellussera in Veneto, while tourism demand and DO harvest rules protect pergolas in Galicia.

EU quality labels guarantee origin and method for European sparkling wines

  • EU Quality Labels, PDO, PGI, and Organic certifications guarantee sparkling wines' origin, production method, and environmental standards to consumers.
  • PDO sparkling wines like Champagne, Prosecco, Cava, and Austrian Kamptal require all production steps within a designated region using traditional methods.

  • EU Organic certification cannot appear on U.S. wine labels due to lack of mutual recognition with USDA, though European organic farming practices still apply.

0327 JUL 2026

Vineyards deploy shading nets to cool fruit zones as heat intensifies

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and Italy are testing vineyard netting to lower cluster temperatures and preserve color and acidity during heat events, moving beyond traditional bird and hail protection.
  • A 2022 UC Davis study on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters retained 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35°C, the threshold for color degradation.
  • At Petra estate in Tuscany, Merlot clusters shaded with nets measured 40-41°C versus 50°C unshaded; sunburn fell from 41 percent to three percent under 90 percent shade in one trial.
  • Net performance depends on color and placement: white and green nets outperform black ones, and contact with heated fabric can burn grapes; in Barolo, one estate reduced peak fruit-zone temperature from 49.2°C to 43°C with an open net on south-facing rows only.
  • The strategy requires balance: filtered light preserves phenolic development and site character, but excessive shade can trap heat, limit airflow, alter spray penetration, and increase disease pressure.
0413 JUL 2026

Grape sunburn damage spreads as heat waves reach cooler regions

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response to excess light and heat that degrades chlorophyll, polyphenols, and aromatic compounds, producing flavors of cooked fruit, asphalt, and creosote.
  • Berry surface temperatures can spike 22 to 27 degrees Fahrenheit above air temperature in direct sun; Chardonnay shows damage within five minutes above 104 to 109°F under high light.
  • Two forms exist: sunburn browning causes discoloration; sunburn necrosis kills cells, cracks skins, and shrivels berries, driven primarily by extreme heat rather than light alone.
  • Defoliation timing now matters critically; removing leaves at veraison increases sunburn risk, while earlier removal at flowering allows berries to build photoprotective compounds.
  • Producers in Provence and Australia are abandoning mid-summer leaf removal, applying kaolin clay films to cool canopies by up to nine degrees Celsius, and redesigning trellising to shade fruit.
055 AUG 2026

Global alcohol deaths fall 26% since 2010, analysis finds

  • Alcohol-attributable deaths worldwide dropped 26% between 2010 and 2024, according to analysis by the International Alliance for Responsible Drinking using Global Burden of Disease data.
  • Disability-adjusted life years linked to alcohol fell 23% over the same period, while measured high alcohol use exposure declined 10%.
  • The International Alliance for Responsible Drinking attributes the improvement to combined effects of changing drinking patterns, healthcare improvements, public health initiatives, and broader social development, not single interventions.
  • The 26% reduction exceeds the UN's 2010 target of 20% harm reduction by 2030, though gains vary significantly by country and region.
  • Global averages mask regional differences; continued progress is not guaranteed without sustained effort, says Julian Braithwaite, president and CEO of the International Alliance for Responsible Drinking.
