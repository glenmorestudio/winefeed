Grape growers idle vineyards to cut costs while awaiting market recovery

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced annual management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some halving expenses.
  • Vineyard idling, or mothballing for one to three years, lets producers preserve vines and sunk capital when they cannot sell fruit, rather than remove acreage permanently.
  • Idled vines require careful maintenance including adequate irrigation, severe pruning, weed control, and selective fungicide application to survive and return to production within two years.

Early harvests from heat shift winemaking beyond the cellar

  • Domaine Lafage in Roussillon picked grapes on July 23, weeks earlier than ever before, while California Central Coast harvests arrived six to eight weeks ahead of the historical norm.
  • Early harvest now demands more refrigeration during peak electricity prices, strains labour and bottling facilities, and shrinks the window for direct-to-consumer wine sales due to summer heat damaging shipments.
  • Heat also affects labelling adhesion, insurance costs, and irrigation fees, while reduced summer wine-tourism visits in hot regions like Paso Robles offset potential gains from autumn harvest overlaps with family travel season.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna showcase compelling terroir

  • Volcanic soils from past eruptions in Soave, Santorini, the Canary Islands, Hungary, Oregon's Willamette Valley, Auvergne, and Lake County create complex terroir that shapes wine flavor through high acidity, salinity, and savory notes.
  • Volcanic terrain presents farming challenges including rugged heat and wind, but resists pests and disease, enabling organic viticulture and preservation of old indigenous grape varieties.
  • Regional volcanic characteristics produce distinct wine styles: Santorini's Assyrtiko gains mineral intensity; Tokaji Aszú gains saline edginess; Oregon's Pinot Noir develops red fruit qualities from Jory soil; Lake County reds gain grippy tannins and acidity.
