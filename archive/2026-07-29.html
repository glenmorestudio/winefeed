RNDC files Chapter 11 after months of asset sales and supplier losses

  • Republic National Distributing Company filed for voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 26 July, listing liabilities between $1 billion and $10 billion and over 100,000 creditors.
  • The filing follows RNDC's withdrawal from California in June 2025 and sale of 11 markets to Reyes Beverage Group and other competitors, which the company said preserved over 5,000 jobs.

Fine wine markups at luxury hotels have tripled in 60 years, except for famous brands

  • Patrick Schmitt MW compared Gleneagles Hotel's wine list from 1965 to Andrew Fairlie restaurant's current list, adjusting historic prices by 25× for inflation.
  • Krug Champagne markup fell from 80-150% in 1965 to 35% today, suggesting recognisable brands face consumer price-checking that caps restaurant margins.
  • Pol Roger markup expanded from 76% in 1965 to 146% today; German Spätlese markup roughly tripled from 45% to 124%, showing less-familiar wines bear steeper premiums.
  • Château Latour and d'Yquem markups roughly doubled, with Latour rising from 95% to 195% and d'Yquem from 67-94% to 273% on a half bottle.
  • Rosé status inverted: in 1965 a Provençal rosé cost more than premier cru Burgundy at Gleneagles; today it sits unmarked in a regional France section.

Early mowing preserves cover crops without water stress in French organic vineyards

  • Researchers at seven organic vineyards in Occitanie, southern France tested four cover crop management techniques over 2022 to 2024 to balance soil health against vine water competition.
  • Early intervention using mower and superficial tillage maintained cover crop benefits through winter while causing no water stress or yield loss, unlike late-season management.
  • Late-season interventions and roller-crimper methods all reduced yields, berry weight and vine vigour compared to bare-soil control treatment.
  • The technique works because early mowing removes competing plants before summer drought while preserving soil structure through wet winter months in Mediterranean climates.
  • Results are context-dependent on soil type and microclimate, but the study suggests timed cover crop management could help organic vineyards adapt to increasing drought risk.

Vineyards deploy shade netting to cool ripening fruit during heat spikes

  • Shade netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is now deployed to lower cluster temperature and preserve color, acidity, and phenolics during heat events.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded controls when anthocyanins degrade above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • At Petra estate in Tuscany, Merlot clusters under 90 percent shade nets measured 40-41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees in controls, cutting sunburn from 41 percent to three percent.
  • Net color matters: white and green nets perform better than black nets, which absorb and re-emit heat onto fruit; nets must be kept away from clusters to avoid direct contact burn.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses light netting to filter direct sun into brief pulses rather than sustained exposure, balancing phenolic development with heat stress mitigation across multiple seasons.

Grape sunburn spreads as heat waves reach new regions, forcing vineyard practice changes

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response to excess light, ultraviolet radiation, and high berry-surface temperature, which can reach 22 to 27 degrees Fahrenheit above air temperature under direct sun.
  • Two forms of damage occur: sunburn browning, mild yellowing or browning of skin, and sunburn necrosis, lethal cell death where skins crack and berries shrivel, driven primarily by extreme temperature.
  • Sunburned grapes produce wine with flattened fruit, cooked and raisin flavors, astringent tannins, high alcohol, flabby acid, and notes of asphalt, creosote, and rubber that worsen with age.
  • Producers in warm regions are reversing traditional canopy management: delaying or eliminating mid-summer leaf removal, limiting defoliation to one side of vines, and applying reflective kaolin clay to cool canopies by up to nine degrees Celsius.
  • Chêne Bleu in Ventoux and Tyrrell's in Hunter Valley have eliminated summer leaf removal on white varieties entirely after recent heat waves caused significant sunburn damage in 2023 and prior years.
