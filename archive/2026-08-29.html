Devastating Spanish wildfires threaten Sierra de Gredos wine revival

  • Wildfires between July 22-26 destroyed 77,000 hectares across Ávila and Madrid provinces, which contain 85 percent of Sierra de Gredos's 2,000 vineyard hectares.

  • Comando G co-founder Daniel Landi's Las Iruelas single-vineyard bottling in Cebreros suffered heavy smoke exposure and will likely not be produced this vintage.
  • Local producers including Landi and Juanan Martín of Rico Nuevo have submitted proposals to regional authorities requesting firebreak subsidies, vineyard planting support, and dry-stone wall restoration.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna offer complex terroir and distinctive styles

  • Volcanic soils, layered by ancient eruptions across different regions, create wines marked by high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors rather than fruit-forward profiles.
  • Santorini's Assyrtiko whites and Tokaji Aszú sweet wines from Hungary demonstrate how volcanic terroir expresses differently across grape varieties and styles.
  • Volcanic terrain naturally resists pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preservation of old indigenous vines; Santorini holds vines over 200 years old.
  • The Canary Islands' isolation from phylloxera due to volcanic disruptions preserved native varieties like Listán Blanco and Listán Negro, later revived by winemakers in the 1980s and 1990s.
  • Willamette Valley's Jory volcanic soil in Dundee Hills and Eola-Amity Hills AVAs yields red-fruit-forward Pinot Noirs, contrasting with marine sediment expressions elsewhere in the region.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41 Percent of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • Xylella fastidiosa, a bacterium that causes Pierce's disease by blocking water transport in vines, has infected grapevines in Portugal, Spain, Italy, and France since 2016.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards are now at climate risk, up from 21.8 percent in previous models.
  • Vineyard microclimates near river basins and valleys are especially vulnerable because they connect otherwise isolated areas where winter temperatures historically kept the pathogen in check.
  • California growers and researchers are sharing decades of experience managing Pierce's disease with European producers, though management strategies must be tailored to local conditions.
  • Early detection and rapid diagnostics remain critical: the bacterium may spread silently before symptoms appear, creating a false sense of security among growers.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide use by up to 80 percent

  • PIWI grapes, hybrid varieties bred for powdery and downy mildew resistance, reduce fungicide sprays from eight to 15 times yearly to two to three times, as demonstrated at Famille Ducourt in Bordeaux.
  • Modern backcrossing now allows PIWI varieties to contain up to 95 percent Vitis vinifera genetic material, preserving wine quality while retaining fungal resistance from non-vinifera species.
  • Genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, accelerates disease-resistance breeding by scanning 2,000 genetic markers across thousands of vines in days instead of months.
  • Germany led European adoption with 4,000 hectares of PIWI plantings in 2025, a 10 percent increase, while French breeders develop ResDur varieties that maintain regional signatures like Bordeaux character.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study showed PIWI varieties delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental impact reduction compared to traditional varieties, chiefly through lower spraying and improved soil health.
