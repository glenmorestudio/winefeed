Silver Scope Media bids $400k for bankrupt Rogue Ales' recipes and trademarks

  • Silver Scope Media Ltd, a U.K. digital marketing agency, made a $400,000 bid for Rogue Ales & Spirits' intellectual property nine months after parent company Oregon Brewing filed Chapter 7 bankruptcy.

  • A hearing on the IP sale and objections is scheduled for Aug. 26; Rogue shuttered operations citing $17 million in debts against roughly $5 million in assets.

Texas High Plains grape growers struggle as wine sales soften after pandemic boom

  • Texas wineries bought grapes aggressively from 2020 to early 2023 as pandemic-driven home consumption surged, then demand softened and inventories backed up, leaving growers with unsold fruit.
  • High Plains growers now face uneven market terms: multi-year contracts have become rare, some wineries wait for harvest to buy cheaper spot-market fruit, forcing farmers to tier their vineyard care by buyer certainty.
  • A March 2025 freeze damaged yields across High Plains and Hill Country, particularly white varieties, which growers and winemakers view as a painful but potentially stabilizing reset for oversupply.
  • Some growers are professionalizing buyer relationships through formal contracts with deposits, while others including Connor Wilmeth at Lahey Vineyards are exploring East Coast distribution to reduce dependence on local Texas markets.
  • Veteran growers including Jet Wilmeth, Andy Timmons and Neal Newsom are considering retirement or reduced operations, raising succession concerns for wineries that depend on their benchmark vineyard sites.

Historic vine-training systems decline as labor costs and mechanization reshape European wine regions

  • Alberata vine-training in Italy, hoyos on Lanzarote, bellussera near Venice, and emparrado in Rías Baixas all face pressure from high manual labor demands and mechanization incentives.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo vineyards shrank 19 percent between 2000 and 2026, dropping from 2,326 to 1,889 hectares, as labor costs drive growers toward conventional irrigated systems.
  • Tourism sustains hoyos on Lanzarote; Alberto González Plasencia of Bodegas Timanfaya warns that disappearing vineyards would reduce visitor appeal and wine value.
  • Familia Torres adapted machinery to pergola training in Rías Baixas, cutting labor demands; Stefano Quaggio notes bellussera vines near Venice may gain value for climate resilience and wine tourism.
  • Regional survival depends on local economics: Lanzarote introduced subsidies; Veneto's flat terrain favored mechanizable systems; only systems integrating heritage, tourism, or climate advantage retain grower commitment.

Soju surges past shōchū in US market, driven by Korean culture and flavor trends

  • Soju grew 133 percent between 2019 and 2025 to under 1 percent of US spirits volume, while shōchū grew only 11 percent over the same period, says Koryn Ternes, IWSR consulting director for the Americas.

  • Younger US consumers drawn to Korean cuisine, K-pop, and K-drama are trial-purchasing soju for its fruity flavors, low ABV under 20 percent, and affordable price, says Jun-Jun Vichaikul, co-owner of Chicago's Konbini & Kanpai.

RNDC files for bankruptcy after shedding US distribution markets

  • Republic National Distributing Company, formerly the second-largest US wine and spirits distributor, filed for chapter 11 bankruptcy in Texas yesterday to wind down remaining operations and seek buyers for surviving markets.
  • RNDC lists liabilities between $1 billion and $10 billion against assets of $500 million to $1 billion, with over 100,000 creditors and $300 million in top unsecured claims led by $93.92 million owed to Proximo Spirits.
  • The distributor shed most US markets in recent months, selling to Reyes Beverage Group, Columbia Distributing, Martignetti Companies, and BreakThru Beverage Group after withdrawing from California last June.
  • Roughly 1,450 workers remain employed; smaller suppliers with unsecured claims face risk of nonpayment as leverage-poor vendors lack alternatives to RNDC for market access.
  • The bankruptcy excludes National Distributing Company, which merged with Republic Distributing in 2007, and most joint ventures in six states; only the Alaska joint venture is included.

Light exposure and vine vigor shape pyrazine levels in wine grapes

  • Methoxypyrazines, nitrogen-based compounds that create bell pepper and grassiness, accumulate early in berry development then decline sharply at veraison, possibly to deter animals before seeds mature.
  • Light exposure is the primary driver of pyrazine concentration; removing shoots and leaves to increase cluster sunlight reduces pyrazines, as the compounds degrade when exposed to light.
  • Vine vigor and canopy density determine cluster shade; rootstocks imparting high vigor create dense canopies that trap shade and raise pyrazine levels, while low-vigor rootstocks reduce them.
  • Soil type affects pyrazine concentration indirectly through its impact on vine vigor; well-drained gravel soils produce lower pyrazines than water-retentive limestone or clay-silt soils in Bordeaux climates.
  • Temperature shows a correlation with lower pyrazine intensity in warmer sites, though the mechanism remains unclear and is difficult to isolate from light's simultaneous effects.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent

  • PIWIs, hybrid grapes bred from American fungus-resistant species and European Vitis vinifera, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties.
  • Modern genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019 via the US VitisGen initiative, identifies resistance genes with precision and cuts seedling screening time from months to days.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit over traditional varieties, mainly through reduced spraying, chemical, fuel, and labor costs.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, four percent of total vineyard area, up ten percent year-on-year; France develops region-specific ResDur hybrids retaining Bordeaux character.
  • Direct-to-consumer sales dominate PIWI distribution at 71 percent in Germany; retail expansion depends on demonstrating mature vine performance and competitive pricing alongside sustainability.

Vineyards deploy netting to cool grape clusters as heat stress intensifies

  • UC Davis 2022 trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit during heat stress, showing netting can prevent color degradation above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses light hail netting on 90 percent of vineyards to filter intense high-altitude sun into brief pulses rather than continuous exposure, reducing vine stress in dry conditions.
  • Marco Simonit's 2025 field trials at Petra estate in Tuscany showed Merlot sunburn dropped from 41 percent in unshaded control to three percent under 90 percent shade netting, with cluster temperatures falling from 50 to 40 degrees Celsius.
  • Boroli in Barolo's Villero vineyard found that net color matters; an open net reduced peak temperatures from 49.2 to 43 degrees Celsius, while denser netting exceeded 50 degrees by trapping heat against fruit.
  • Netting creates tradeoffs: while it protects berries during heat, it can compress canopies, limit airflow, alter spray penetration, and increase disease pressure if applied too densely or touching fruit.

02The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·12 AUG 2026

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • Loire vigneron Sébastien David was ordered to destroy 2,000+ bottles of Cabernet Franc in 2019 for exceeding the EU's 1.2 g/L volatile acidity limit; a petition against the decision gathered nearly 175,000 signatures.
  • The EU volatile acidity limit dates to 1979 and was revised in 1987 with vague consumer-protection language, but the US allows 1.4 g/L for red wines and up to 1.7 g/L for super-ripe grapes.
  • The EU's 2025 exceptions list permits volatile acidity up to 2.4 g/L in hundreds of appellations across 18 countries, including Amarone, Madeira, and Tokaji aszú, contradicting the standard 1.2 g/L red wine limit.
  • Volatile acidity becomes unsafe only above 10% concentration; the EU limit of 1.2 g/L sits between safe wine and vinegar's minimum 4 g/L threshold with no established health risk.
  • Orange wines must conform to the white wine limit of 1.08 g/L despite having tannin and volatile acidity levels similar to reds, creating an inconsistent regulatory standard.
