Restaurant beverage directors navigate tariffs, supply chaos, rising costs

  • Republic National Distributing Company's collapse into Chapter 11 bankruptcy destabilized US beverage wholesale supply, forcing directors to seek new vendors and stockpile strategically.
  • Will Jones, wine director at The Hope Farm in Fairhoe, Ala., substitutes unavailable Premier Cru Burgundy with Hautes-Côtes de Beaune or Austrian wines rather than raising prices beyond customer tolerance.
  • Felipe de Assis Villela, beverage director at Bluepoint Hospitality Group in Easton, Md., buys in bulk and moderates markups below industry standard 200-300 percent to move inventory and balance profit with guest experience.

Hungarian dry wines gain US sommelier interest, but consumer awareness lags

Arnaldo Rivera, a top tier of wines from the overperforming Terre del Barolo cooperative

  • Terre del Barolo cooperative, founded in 1958 by Arnaldo Rivera, produces 12% of all Barolo from 240 of the appellation's 2200 hectares with 300 growers.
  • Arnaldo Rivera, the top tier representing 10% of cooperative production, is a vineyard-designated project launched ten years ago with 35 contributing growers paid by site potential yield.
  • The cooperative uses large-format wooden casks traditionally; for Arnaldo Rivera's small vineyard plots, they carefully source smaller oak with vapor-bent staves, avoiding toasting to minimize wine impact.

How Can You Train to Recognise Wine Faults?

  • Sietze Wijma founded The Art of Tasting in 2021, teaching wine professionals and enthusiasts to recognize faults using synthesized flavor compounds; the company now has 30,000 Instagram followers.
  • Wijma launched an Online Tasting Course in wine faults after working three years at a UK laboratory producing aroma compounds, then starting his own business during Covid.

Red and White and Roero All Over

  • Roero, north of Alba in Piemonte, produces white Arneis and red Nebbiolo on sandstone-based terrain distinct from the Langhe, with 1,000 planted hectares of white versus 360 of red.
  • Roero Nebbiolo is approachable with limber tannins and bright acidity; Arneis is peachy, floral, and saline, priced $25, $45 for reds and under $25 for whites in the U.S.
  • Some Roero producers label reds as Langhe Nebbiolo or Nebbiolo d'Alba to leverage laxer production rules and more recognizable names rather than the Roero D.O.C.G. designation.
