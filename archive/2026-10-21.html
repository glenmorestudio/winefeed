02IVES Open Science·21 OCT 2026

Surface renewal method estimates vineyard water use on Napa slopes

  • Researchers estimated water loss from flat and sloped Napa Valley vineyards using surface renewal, calibrated against eddy covariance measurements.
  • Average June-September evapotranspiration was 2.46 mm per day in the flat vineyard, 2.11 in the northeast slope, and 2.54 in the southwest slope.
  • Water use was lower on the northeast slope, which the authors attribute to reduced canopy resistance from increased late-season shading.
Reported byIVES Open Science: Surface renewal method estimates vineyard water use on Napa slopes
03IVES Open Science·21 OCT 2026

ABA and heat boost anthocyanins in Cabernet Sauvignon berries after veraison

  • Argentine researchers Liliana Deis, María Fernanda Silva and Jorge Cavagnaro tested abscisic acid (ABA) and temperature on Cabernet Sauvignon berries in vitro.
  • ABA did not raise anthocyanins or total polyphenols before veraison, but after veraison it increased both compounds.
  • At 23 days after veraison, ABA raised anthocyanins by 207% to 307% and total polyphenols by 271% to 428%, depending on dose.

04IVES Open Science·21 OCT 2026

Pre-bloom leaf removal cuts Sangiovese yields and lifts sugar and colour

  • University of Bologna researchers tested pre-bloom hand and mechanical basal leaf removal on Sangiovese vines against undefoliated controls.
  • Both defoliation methods lowered fruit-set, yield per shoot, cluster weight and berry weight.
  • Soluble solids and total anthocyanins improved with both methods, and mechanical removal kept most of hand removal's benefits.

Seven Vermentino selections compared for yield and fruit quality in Sardinia

  • Researchers from the University of Sassari and LAORE Sardegna tested seven Vermentino selections over two seasons in a Northern Sardinian vineyard.

10IVES Open Science·21 OCT 2026

Barley cover crops in vine rows cut erosion and runoff nutrients in California trial

  • A 2005 University of California Cooperative Extension trial in Salinas compared in-row barley cover crops with herbicide-treated and bare middles.
  • Barley cover crops reduced soil and water erosion from winter rains and lowered nutrient content in runoff compared with bare soil.
  • Cover crops killed before bud break did not reduce vine growth or yield, while those allowed to grow past bud break cut growth without cutting yields.
