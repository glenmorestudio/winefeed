Seven volcanic wine regions worldwide rival Mount Etna's mineral complexity

  • Volcanic soils from past eruptions create complex, layered terroirs in Soave (Italy), Santorini (Greece), the Canary Islands, Hungary's Tokaji region, Oregon's Willamette Valley, Auvergne (France), and Lake County (California).
  • Volcanic terroir produces signature flavors: high acidity, salinity, and savory notes dominate over fruit-forward profiles across these regions and grape varieties.
  • Volcanic soils resist pests and disease naturally, enabling organic farming and preserving centuries-old indigenous vines untouched by phylloxera in areas like the Canary Islands.
  • Challenging growing conditions, heat, wind, rocky terrain, require unconventional techniques such as basket-trained vines in Santorini and hand-dug individual planting holes in Lanzarote.
  • Mineral content from volcanic geology adds distinctive edginess even to sweet wines, exemplified by Hungarian Tokaji Aszú's saline qualities that distinguish it from other dessert wines.

03SevenFifty Daily·17 AUG 2026

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41% of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • Pierce's disease, caused by Xylella fastidiosa bacterium and spread by glassy-winged sharpshooter insects, devastated California's Temecula Valley in the late 1990s, costing growers $48 million annually.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2% of European vineyards now face climate risk of Pierce's disease, up from 21.8% in previous models, driven by warming winters.
  • Vineyard microclimates near river basins and valleys are especially vulnerable because these topographically complex areas connect otherwise isolated zones where the bacterium can now survive.
  • Pierce's disease was detected in European grapevines on Mallorca in 2016 and in continental Europe in Portugal in 2023; cases persist in regulated zones across Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • California's decades-long experience shows early detection, vector management, and sustained research are critical, though Europe lacks coordinated communication strategies between regional bodies and growers.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide use by up to 80 percent

  • PIWIs, fungus-resistant hybrid grapes bred from European and American vine species, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties.
  • Genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision, accelerating breeding of new resistant varieties.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, representing 10 percent annual growth and four percent of total German vineyard area.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit reduction compared to traditional varieties, mainly through lower spraying costs.
  • Bordeaux University and French researchers are developing ResDur hybrids crossing PIWI traits with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot to retain regional signature while gaining disease resistance.
