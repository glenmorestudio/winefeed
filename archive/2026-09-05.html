Seven US sommeliers reveal wine markup ranges from 60 to 76 percent of bill

  • Seven beverage directors across US restaurants report markups ranging 60-76 percent of final menu price, with most allocating 35-48 percent as actual profit after operational costs.
  • Kinga Mackowiak, beverage director at Apple Blossom in Denver, marks up bottles 60-70 percent but varies margins by demand and regional familiarity rather than applying uniform multipliers.
  • William Jones at Deep Roots Restaurant Group in Fairhoe, Alabama reports 76 percent average markup but says tariffs and labor costs now make it harder to hit profit targets.
  • Beverage directors cite glassware, labor, insurance, rent, and utilities as hidden costs justifying markups; sommelier June Rodil says guests pay for storage, temperature control, and service expertise.
  • Restaurant alcohol profit contribution dropped from 60 percent of total profits to roughly 30 percent as US drinking rates fall and economic pressures reduce on-premise wine consumption.

Early harvests disrupt wine sales, tourism, and supply chains across regions

  • Grape harvests in Roussillon, California, Champagne, and Germany arrived weeks or months early in 2024, with Domaine Lafage in France picking July 23 for the first time ever.
  • Aaron Jackson, winemaker at Aaron Wines in Paso Robles, reports July tasting-room visits drop sharply as heat rises, and shipping blackout periods for direct-to-consumer sales are expanding.
  • European producers including Šárka Betke at Lacery vineyard in Czech Moravia face delayed payments when summer heat forces shipments into fall, straining cash flow for established wineries.
  • Early August harvests force wineries to refrigerate grapes during peak electricity prices instead of cool October, raising costs; label adhesion and bottling-line capacity also strain under compressed schedules.
  • Wine tourism to Champagne and other regions may shift into November as fall weather improves, but earlier harvests mean fewer winemakers available to host visitors during peak summer travel season.

Federal THC limit ban upends booming hemp-drink retail market

  • Art Nergaryan's four Massachusetts wine shops saw sales jump 25-35 percent after stocking THC-infused drinks, until state regulators banned them from wine stores in May 2024.
  • THC-infused beverage retail sales reached $239 million in the 52 weeks prior to April 2026, a 135 percent increase, but the market faces potential collapse.
  • Congress passed legislation in November 2025 capping hemp-derived THC at 0.4 milligrams per container, effective November 12, 2026, effectively banning most commercial hemp drinks.
  • State rules contradict federal law: Minnesota and Georgia permit hemp beverages, while California, Washington, and Vermont ban them; the FDA calls all interstate THC drinks unlawful.
  • Industry members including retailers, distributors, and manufacturers lack clarity on which products remain legal to sell, with enforcement deadlines approaching in late 2026.

American single malt ratification fails to lift struggling category

  • The US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau approved a standard of identity for American single malt in December 2024 after a decade of petitioning by distillers via the American Single Malt Whiskey Commission.
  • Major producers including Westland Distillery in Seattle, Balcones Distillery in Waco Texas, and Westward Whiskey in Portland have laid off staff or ceased production amid weak demand and economic headwinds.
  • Ratification has helped retailers create dedicated shelf space and improved export tracking, but consumer awareness remains limited outside connoisseur circles despite releases from Jim Beam, Bulleit, and Jack Daniel's.
  • Distillers are adapting by launching lower-priced cask-finished variants, blending single malt with bourbon, and diversifying into rye, tequila, and other spirits to drive growth.

Seven volcanic wine regions rival Mount Etna's mineral complexity

  • Volcanic soils worldwide create wines marked by high acidity, salinity, and savory rather than fruity flavors, distinct from non-volcanic regions.
  • Santorini's Assyrtiko and Tokaji Aszú showcase how volcanic minerality elevates wines: Santorini produces unmatched depth and acidity; Tokaji gains saline edge that distinguishes it from other sweet wines.
  • Canary Islands winemakers revived indigenous grapes like Listán Blanco and Listán Negro from the 1980s onward, preserved because phylloxera never arrived and volcanic isolation shielded native varieties.
  • Oregon's Willamette Valley volcanic Jory soils yield red fruit Pinot Noirs, contrasting marine sediment's darker expression; Jory was named Oregon's state soil in 2011.
  • Lake County, California and Auvergne, France show volcanic terroir favors organic and natural winemaking: Lake County's high elevation produces grippy Cabernet Sauvignon; Auvergne's Gamay d'Auvergne yields darker, spicier wines than other French Gamays.

Pierce's Disease Spreads in Europe as Climate Warms; California Offers Hard-Won Lessons

  • Pierce's disease, caused by bacterium Xylella fastidiosa, killed large blocks of Barbera, Chardonnay, and Riesling in California's Temecula Valley from the late 1990s onward, costing growers over $48 million annually.
  • The bacterium was detected in European grapevines in Mallorca, Spain in 2016 and in continental Europe in Portugal in 2023, with cases now managed in regulated zones across Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 climate study found 41.2 percent of European vineyards face climate risk of Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, with river basins and valleys especially vulnerable as winters warm.
  • California researchers including Olakunle Olawole at UC Riverside advise Europe that early detection, vector management, and genomic surveillance can slow spread once eradication becomes infeasible at scale.
  • European growers and policymakers lack coordinated reporting and communication systems, risking concealment of infections; California's decades-long response shows coordination and early action minimize long-term damage.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes slash fungicide use by up to 80 percent

  • PIWIs, fungus-resistant hybrid grape varieties bred by crossing non-vinifera and vinifera vines, reduce fungicide sprays from 8-15 times yearly to 2-3 times for powdery and downy mildew.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by VitisGen in 2019, identifies precise disease-resistance genes and enables marker-assisted selection, reducing breeding time from months to days.
  • Germany leads adoption with 4,000 hectares of PIWI vines in 2025, representing 4 percent of national vineyard area and 10 percent annual growth.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental benefit reduction, primarily through lower spraying costs and reduced soil toxicity.
  • France is developing ResDur varieties, crossing PIWIs with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot to retain Bordeaux character while adding fungal resistance for experimental planting.

03Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, then are disgorged and dosed; they're nutty and complex from aging protocols.
  • Ancestral method sparkling wines ferment in bottle before drying, retain lees, and remain fully dry and lightly cloudy; the method predates traditional method winemaking.
  • Tank-method sparkling wines ferment in pressurized tanks with added sugar and yeast, producing clean and direct styles with limited lees contact.
  • Skin-contact wines macerate white grapes with skins, seeds, and pulp during fermentation, creating color, tannin, and flavor; the tradition originated in the Caucasus.
  • Botrytis cinerea fungus concentrates grape sugars by shriveling berries, creating honeyed and lightly savory flavors in still white wines.
