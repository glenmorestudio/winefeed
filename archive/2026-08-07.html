0227 JUL 2026

Vineyards deploy shade netting to cool fruit zones in extreme heat

  • Shade netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is now deployed to lower fruit-zone temperatures and preserve acidity, color, and phenolics during heat events.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon showed shaded clusters retained 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius during heat spikes.
  • At Catena Zapata in Mendoza and Petra in Tuscany, trials found white or green nets outperformed black nets, and placement matters: nets touching grapes cause sunburn, while one-sided shading prevents heat-trapping oven effects.
  • Boroli in Barolo reduced maximum fruit-zone temperature from 49.2 to 43 degrees Celsius with open-mesh netting, but denser shade nets paradoxically reached 50 degrees, proving more coverage does not guarantee cooler fruit.
  • Growers now balance shading against phenolic development and disease risk, treating the fruit zone as a precision microclimate rather than a simple protection zone.

Grape sunburn spreads beyond hot regions as heat waves arrive earlier

  • Grape sunburn is a stress response when berry surface temperatures exceed 104-109°F under high light, causing chlorophyll and polyphenols to degrade and producing flavors of leather, asphalt, and creosote in finished wine.
  • Direct sun can heat exposed berries 22-27°F above air temperature, meaning a 95°F day creates berry surfaces above 122°F on the sun-facing side.
  • Producers in Provence and Australia have reversed decades of practice, abandoning or delaying leaf removal in summer and avoiding east-side defoliation to protect fruit from sudden heat waves.
  • Tyrrell's in Australia's Hunter Valley has used kaolin clay spray since 2014, cooling canopy by up to 9°C, as a preventive alternative to leaf removal.
  • Winemakers including Steve Matthiasson in Napa now design vineyards to shade fruit and sort sunburned berries at harvest, recognizing that direct sun damages color, aroma, and acid.

Global alcohol deaths fall 26% since 2010, analysis finds

  • Alcohol-attributable deaths worldwide fell 26% between 2010 and 2024, according to analysis by the International Alliance for Responsible Drinking of Global Burden of Disease data.
  • Disability-adjusted life years linked to alcohol fell 23% over the same period, while high alcohol use exposure declined 10%.
  • The improvement likely reflects combined effects of changing drinking patterns, healthcare improvements, public health initiatives, and broader social development rather than single interventions.
