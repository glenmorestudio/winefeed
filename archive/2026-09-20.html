California and Pacific Northwest vineyards idle production to cut costs amid weak demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary pause in production lasting one to three years, allows producers to avoid removal and preserve assets while awaiting market recovery.
  • Idled vines require careful management including minimal irrigation, aggressive pruning, weed control, and selective spraying to survive dormancy and resume quality production.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna showcase terroir shaped by ancient eruptions

  • Volcanic soils from ancient eruptions in Soave, Santorini, the Canary Islands, Hungary, Oregon's Willamette Valley, Auvergne, and California's Lake County create mineral-driven wines with high acidity and savory flavors.
  • Volcanic terrain offers practical advantages: soils resist pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preservation of old indigenous grape varieties, while protecting vines from harsh climates.
  • Wine character varies by geology: Soave's Garganega expresses orchard fruit and acidity; Santorini's Assyrtiko delivers mineral depth; Willamette's volcanic Jory soil yields red fruit Pinot Noirs; Lake County's elevation and soils produce tannic, acidic reds.

  • Global port wine sales declined 25% over the last two decades; the Douro now produces as much table wine as port, with table wine as the region's economic foundation.
