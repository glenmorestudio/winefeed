EU Quality Labels Help Consumers Identify Authentic Sparkling Wines

  • The European Union certifies sparkling wines with PDO, PGI, and Organic labels to guarantee origin, production method, quality, and sustainability practices.
  • PDO sparkling wines like Champagne, Prosecco, Cava, and Austrian Sekt must be fully produced within a single designated region using traditional methods.

Early mowing preserves cover crops without stressing vines in drought

  • Researchers at seven organic vineyards in Occitanie, France tested four methods of managing service crops between vine rows over 2022-2024.
  • Early intervention using a mower combined with shallow tillage, timed around budburst, maintained soil benefits without reducing yield or causing water stress.
  • Late interventions and roller-crimper methods all reduced yields, berry weight and vine vigour compared to bare soil control.
  • The technique addresses competition between cover crops and vines for water during Mediterranean dry summers while preserving soil anchoring through winter.
  • Results are context-dependent on soil type and microclimate, but suggest a timing-based solution for organic vineyards adapting to heatwaves.

Vineyard netting emerges as climate tool to cool grape clusters during heat

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and France are using shade netting to lower cluster-zone temperatures and preserve berry color and acidity during heat events, moving beyond traditional bird and hail protection.
  • A 2022 UC Davis trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent greater anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded the 35-degree threshold where color compounds degrade.
  • At Petra estate in Tuscany, netting reduced Merlot cluster temperatures from roughly 50 degrees Celsius to 40-41 degrees, dropping sunburn incidence from 40-41 percent to as low as three percent under 90 percent shade.
  • Net color and placement matter critically: white and green nets perform better than black, and nets must be kept away from clusters to avoid heat reflection; Boroli in Barolo found one-sided coverage on south-facing rows prevented trapping heat.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza and other estates are conducting multi-season trials to balance filtered light exposure, needed for phenolic development, against heat stress, testing shade levels from 15 to 90 percent.

Grape sunburn spreads north as heat waves arrive earlier and further

  • Sunburn occurs when berry surface temperatures exceed 104-109°F under high light, causing chlorophyll and aromatic compounds to degrade and producing aromas of asphalt, creosote, and cooked fruit.
  • Joanna Gambetta, wine scientist at University of Newcastle Australia, found that early defoliation at flowering builds photoprotective compounds in berries, while late defoliation at veraison increases sunburn risk.
  • Producers in Provence and Napa are abandoning mid-summer leaf removal and east-side exposure; Tyrrell's in Hunter Valley applies kaolin clay to cool canopy by up to 9°C Celsius.
  • Steve Matthiasson, winemaker at Matthiasson Wines Napa Valley, identifies sunburned grapes by cooked flavors, light color with brown rim, high alcohol, and flabby acid, faults that worsen with age.
  • Laura Iverson at Chêne Bleu in Ventoux stopped all summer leaf removal on white varieties after a 40°C heat wave in 2023 caused shriveling and stalled ripening.
