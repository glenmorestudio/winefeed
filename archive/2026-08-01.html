US wine industry lacks sustained collective marketing unlike dairy and almonds

Australian wine exports hit 22-year low as global demand weakens

  • Wine Australia reported exports fell 7% in value to A$2.3bn and 6% in volume to 598 million litres in the year to June 2026, the lowest volume since 2004.
  • Global wine consumption has fallen to its lowest level since 1961, driven by changing consumer habits, cost-of-living pressures and competition from alternative alcoholic beverages, according to Wine Australia.
  • Exports to Australia's three largest markets declined: mainland China fell 15% to A$756m; UK and US shipments hit 25-year lows, with UK weakness concentrated in lower and mid-priced wines.
  • Canada emerged as a bright spot, with exports rising 20% in value to A$188m, partly due to market-share gains when US wines became scarce during trade disputes.
  • Several Asian markets outside China posted strong growth, with Thailand achieving record export values and Singapore becoming Australia's largest Asian export market outside mainland China.

South Korean men drink less; women over 30 drink more

  • The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency's 2025 Community Health Survey of 230,000 adults found high-risk drinking fell across all male age groups between 2016 and 2025, but rose in every female age group except those in their 20s.

Vineyards deploy shade netting to protect fruit from heat damage during ripening

  • Shade netting, traditionally used to block hail and birds, is now being tested as a climate tool to lower fruit-zone temperatures and preserve color and acidity during heat events.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters retained 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius, the threshold for color degradation.
  • At Catena Zapata in Mendoza, filtered light through canopy leaves is preferred over full sun exposure; trials compare nets providing 17 percent, 50 percent, and no shade to measure effects on vine physiology and wine quality.
  • At Petra in Tuscany, Merlot clusters under 90 percent shade nets measured 40-41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees in unshaded controls, reducing sunburn from 41 percent to three percent in one trial.
  • Net color, placement distance from fruit, and row orientation all affect cooling efficacy; darker nets absorb more heat than white or green ones, and nets touching heated fruit can cause burn instead of preventing it.

Grape sunburn shifts winemaking as heat waves reach new regions

  • Chris Tanghe, MS and director of education for GuildSomm, identified sunburned grapes by their sensory signature: flattened fruit, oxidized bitterness, and notes of asphalt, rubber, and creosote.
  • Sunburn degrades chlorophyll, polyphenols, carotenoids, and terpenes that produce floral and citrus notes, while reducing tartaric acid and anthocyanins in red varieties, according to UC Davis research.
  • Producers including Tyrrell's in Hunter Valley and Chêne Bleu in Ventoux are abandoning mid-summer leaf removal and now use kaolin clay film or limit defoliation to early flowering, when berries can build photoprotective compounds.
  • Steve Matthiasson, winemaker at Matthiasson Wines in Napa Valley, emphasizes vineyard design, canopy architecture, and sorting sunburned grapes as critical defenses as heat waves arrive earlier and further north.

Mourvèdre struggles to stay balanced as southern France grows hotter and drier

  • Mourvèdre, a late-ripening Spanish-origin variety long suited to Bandol's coastal heat, now faces extreme temperatures and drought that threaten even its heat tolerance in southern France.
  • Producers including Jean-François Ott at Domaines Ott and Pauline and Clément Gueissard are using shade-promoting canopy techniques and ground cover to help grapes ripen without sunburn and retain soil moisture.
  • A three-year drought from 2022 to 2024 in Jumilla killed old bush vines despite their deep roots, forcing growers to irrigate and abandon hotter southern parcels in favor of higher-altitude sites.
  • Bandol's cooler sites, traditionally reserved for rosé production, are now being considered for red wine as growers shift to parcels with better water retention and temperature stability.
  • The 2021 vintage showed lower juice-to-skin ratios and earlier harvests than normal, signaling how drought stress is altering ripening patterns and wine composition in the region.
