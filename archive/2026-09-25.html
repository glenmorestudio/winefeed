California and Pacific Northwest vineyards idle acres to cut costs amid weak demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, or mothballing, pauses production for one to three years without removing vines, allowing producers to preserve assets while awaiting demand recovery.
  • Proper idling requires sustained irrigation, aggressive pruning, weed control, and minimal fungicide application to keep vines viable for future production; inadequate care can cause lasting damage.

Early harvests reshape winemaking beyond the cellar as climate shifts peak season.

  • Domaine Lafage in Roussillon picked grapes July 23, weeks to months earlier than tradition; California Central Coast harvests arrived six to eight weeks ahead of normal, the earliest in 27 years for some growers.
