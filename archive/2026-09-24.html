Vineyard operators idle acres to cut costs while awaiting market recovery

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary halt to production lasting one to three years, preserves vines and capital rather than removal, allowing producers to survive current weak grape demand.
  • Viticulturists report idling requires careful maintenance including aggressive pruning, minimal irrigation, weed control, and selective fungicide spraying to revive vines when markets recover.
