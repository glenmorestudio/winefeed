Seven US regions seek AVA status to establish wine identity

  • Seven proposed AVAs span California, Oregon, New Jersey, and New York; approval by the federal Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau typically takes several years.
  • Annapolis-Sea Ranch in Sonoma County will cover 26,637 acres with 651 acres planted, known for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay shaped by coastal fog and Ohlson Ranch soil.
  • Deschutes River in central Oregon will be the state's first recognized winegrowing region at 723,760 acres, with growers testing both traditional European varieties and cold-hardy hybrids.

04The Wine Economist·9 SEP 2026

Costco stocks boutique wines as producers struggle to sell

  • Costco, the largest US wine retailer, now carries boutique producer wines alongside its Kirkland Signature label despite carrying only 100-120 wines at any given time.
  • Producers struggling to sell wine in a surplus market are accepting placement at Costco, allowing the chain to source inventory that would rarely appear in traditional retail.
  • Costco's house brand model mirrors British supermarket strategy, using retailer reputation to sell unfamiliar global wines rather than relying on producer brands like US competitors do.

Grape growers idle vineyards to cut costs as market demand stalls

  • Randy Heinzen, president of Vineyard Professional Services in Paso Robles, California, notes idling is attractive because it preserves capital investment and emotional attachment to land that removal would destroy.

03VinePair·30 SEP 2026

Pink Chardonnay mutation gains traction among natural winemakers and sommeliers

  • Pink Chardonnay, a pink-skinned Chardonnay mutation first documented in Burgundy in the early 1900s, is produced by a handful of winemakers including Stéphane Tissot in Jura and Rajat Parr on California's Central Coast.
  • Champagne approved Pink Chardonnay as a permissible variety last year, and sommeliers including Tira Johnson at New York City restaurants Chez Fifi and Sushi Noz now offer single varietal expressions to diners.
  • Production remains tiny: Tissot yields 2,000 to 4,000 bottles annually of his Rose Massale cuvée, while Parr produces only 20 to 50 cases per vintage, limiting availability largely to direct-to-consumer sales.
