California and Pacific Northwest vineyards idle acreage to cut costs amid weak demand

  • Vineyard idling, a temporary halt to production lasting one to three years, saved northern Willamette Valley producers an average of 38 percent per acre in 2025, with some cutting costs by half.
  • Viticulture managers including those at Mercer Ranches in Washington and Lost Draw Vineyards in Texas are idling selected blocks while evaluating replanting options, as weak grape sales have made older plantings unviable.
  • Proper idling requires sustained irrigation, aggressive pruning, and light disease management to prevent long-term vine damage; vines left unattended risk years of recovery work.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna offer distinct mineral terroirs

  • Volcanic soils across Soave, Santorini, the Canary Islands, Hungary, Oregon's Willamette Valley, Auvergne, and California's Lake County produce wines marked by high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors rather than fruit-forward profiles.
  • Volcanic terrain inhibits pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preserving old indigenous grape varieties; Santorini and the Canary Islands retain vines over 200 years old without phylloxera damage.
  • Each region's volcanic geology shapes distinct styles: Santorini's Assyrtiko gains mineral depth unmatched elsewhere; Tokaji Aszú gains saline edginess from mineral-rich soils; Oregon's Jory soil yields red-fruit Pinot Noir compared to marine-sediment darkness.

Myths of The Douro

  • Global port sales have declined 25% over the last two decades; the Douro now produces equal volumes of table wine and port.
  • The Douro's vineyard grading system historically rewarded sites suited to port production (high sugar, color, tannin), not table wine, skewing the region's reputation toward big, heavy wines.
  • New-generation winemakers like Rita Marques, Luis Seabra, and Pedro Coelho are producing lighter, high-altitude Douro table wines with classical structure and acidity, challenging the region's port-driven legacy.
