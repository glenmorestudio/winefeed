California and Pacific Northwest vineyards idle acres to weather weak grape demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary pause in production lasting one to three years, allows owners to avoid bulldozing vines while preserving capital and waiting for demand to recover.
  • Viticulturists emphasize proper maintenance during idling, including consistent irrigation, aggressive pruning, and disease monitoring, as inadequate care can cause long-term vine damage.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna show terroir's fiery foundation

  • Volcanic soils create complex, layered terroirs hostile to pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preservation of old vine varieties across regions from Santorini to Oregon's Willamette Valley.
  • Wines from volcanic regions share trademark high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors; Santorini's Assyrtiko, Tokaji Aszú, and Willamette Valley Pinot Noir exemplify how lava composition influences taste.
  • The Canary Islands retained phylloxera-free native varieties like Listán Blanco and Listán Negro after volcanic eruptions isolated ports in the 1700s, allowing winemakers to revive them from the 1980s onward.

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo bottle conditioning with sugar and yeast for second fermentation, then disgorgement and dosage, creating nutty and complex styles.
  • Tank method (Charmat/Martinotti) uses pressurized tanks for second fermentation, producing cleaner and fresher sparkling wines than traditional method.
  • Skin-contact wines macerate white grapes with skins during fermentation, creating color, tannin, and texture; the tradition originated in the Caucasus and has been reinvigorated in the last two decades.
