  • Soju grew 133 percent between 2019 and 2025 to less than one percent of U.S. spirits volume, while shōchū expanded only 11 percent over the same period, says Koryn Ternes, IWSR consulting director for the Americas.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41 Percent of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • Pierce's disease, caused by Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, has infected grapevines in Mallorca since 2016 and mainland Europe since 2023, with cases now in Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face climate risk from Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in previous models.
  • Vineyard microclimates in river basins and valleys are especially vulnerable because warming winters no longer suppress the bacterium in these topographically complex areas.
  • California's decades of experience show eradication works early but shifts to slowing spread once the pathogen becomes established; European countries lack coordinated communication strategies to enable early detection.
  • Olakunle Olawole, assistant professor at University of California Riverside, says genomic surveillance and rapid diagnostics can detect the bacterium faster than visual symptoms appear.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent across Europe

  • PIWIs are hybrid grape varieties bred to resist powdery and downy mildew by crossing disease-resistant American vines with European Vitis vinifera, now containing up to 95 percent vinifera genetic material.
  • Jérémy Ducourt, technical director at Famille Ducourt in Bordeaux, reduced fungicide sprays from eight to 15 per season for traditional varieties down to two to three for PIWIs over a decade of planting.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and enables marker-assisted selection across 2,000 genetic markers, reducing breeding time from months to days.
  • French breeders are developing ResDur varieties crossed with Bordeaux signature grapes like Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot for experimental plots starting in 2026, balancing disease resistance with regional identity.
