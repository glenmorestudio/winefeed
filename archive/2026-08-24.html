Ancient vine-training systems vanish as labor costs and mechanization reshape European vineyards

  • Lanzarote's hoyo system, a 18th-century pit method that attracts thousands of tourists annually, has seen active vineyard area shrink 19 percent between 2000 and 2026 due to extreme labor demands.
  • Alberto González Plasencia, owner of Bodegas Timanfaya on Lanzarote, warns that losing hoyos would cut tourist visits and reduce wine value; local governments have introduced subsidies to slow decline.
  • Juan Glaria, winemaker at Bodegas Rubicón, notes hoyo maintenance requires constant manual pit entry and takes very long hours even per hectare, driving some producers to switch to mechanized vineyards.
  • Familia Torres maintains Rías Baixas pergolas by adopting specialized machinery for ground work; regional harvest rules require manual picking anyway, making pergola training economically viable there.
  • Bellussera, a 19th-century pergola hybrid near Venice once supporting silk production, declined to 9 percent of Piave DOC but may revive through wine tourism and superior climate resilience.

Scotland's 154 distilleries span five official regions plus emerging Islands category

  • The Scotch Whisky Association counts 154 malt and grain distilleries across Scotland as of 2026, organized into five official regions: Highlands, Lowlands, Speyside, Islay, and Campbeltown, plus an informal Islands designation.
  • Speyside, technically a subregion of the Highlands, hosts more than 50 distilleries and is recognized as the world's most densely populated whisky region, celebrated for sweet, fruit-forward expressions.
  • Campbeltown, once home to roughly 30 distilleries in the late 1800s and called the Whisky Capital of the World, now operates just three: Springbank, Glen Scotia, and Glengyle.
  • Islay, home to 11 modern distilleries including Ardbeg, Lagavulin, and Laphroaig, is famed for heavily peated smoky malts but also produces unpeated styles from producers like Bunnahabhain.
  • The Islands category, an informal grouping across Arran, Jura, Orkney, Skye, and other islands, has seen rapid growth; Torabhaig opened on Skye in 2017 as the first new legal distillery there in nearly 190 years.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41% of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • A bacterium called Xylella fastidiosa causes Pierce's disease by blocking water transport in grapevines, killing them within years; it was detected in European grapevines in Spain's Mallorca in 2016 and Portugal in 2023.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards are now at climate risk, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, with river valleys and basins acting as hotspots.
  • Warming winters allow the pathogen to survive in regions where cold previously suppressed it; California's experience shows early detection and vector management slow spread once eradication becomes infeasible.
  • European countries lack coordinated communication strategies between regional bodies and growers, creating risks of concealment if vineyard destruction is the only mandated response.
  • California winegrowers and researchers are sharing decades of experience with the disease to help Europe develop tailored management strategies beyond eradication alone.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent across Europe

  • PIWIs, hybrid grapes bred to resist powdery and downy mildew, require two to three fungicide sprays yearly versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties, cutting chemical inputs by up to 80 percent.
  • German breeders developed PIWIs in the late 20th century by backcrossing American disease-resistant vines with European Vitis vinifera; modern PIWIs now contain up to 95 percent vinifera genetic material.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019, allows breeders to pinpoint resistance genes with precision and stack multiple layers of protection in new varieties within days instead of months.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, a 10 percent increase; France is developing ResDur varieties that retain regional character, with Bordeaux crossing PIWI stock with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWI cultivation delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefits versus traditional varieties, chiefly through reduced spraying and lower soil and water toxicity.

Vineyards test netting to cool grape clusters amid intensifying heat

  • UC Davis 2022 trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters retained 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded degradation threshold of 35 degrees Celsius.
  • Luis Reginato, head of viticulture at Catena Zapata in Mendoza, uses canopy management to deliver filtered light pulses rather than direct sun exposure, with 90 percent of vineyards under hail netting.
  • Marco Simonit's 2025 trials at Petra estate in Tuscany showed 90 percent shade nets reduced Merlot sunburn from 41 percent to 3 percent and lowered cluster temperature from 50 to 40-41 degrees Celsius.
  • Net color and positioning matter: white and green nets perform better than black, and fabric must stay away from clusters to prevent heat transfer that acts as a secondary burn source.
  • Achille Boroli at Barolo's Boroli estate found an open net dropped maximum fruit temperature from 49.2 to 43 degrees Celsius, but denser netting backfired, suggesting optimal shade requires calibration per site and vintage.
