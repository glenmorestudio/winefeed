Vineyard managers adopt idling strategy to cut costs amid weak grape demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced annual management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites halving expenses.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary pause in production lasting one to three years, allows producers to avoid permanent removal while waiting for market recovery and grape demand to improve.
  • Proper idling requires careful management including minimal but consistent irrigation, aggressive pruning, and pest control to ensure vines can return to quality production when markets stabilize.
