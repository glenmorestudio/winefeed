Federal THC limit crushes hemp-drink retail surge across US states

  • THC-infused beverage sales rose 135 percent to $239 million in the year to April 2026, driven by retailer adoption in Massachusetts, Minnesota, Georgia, and Tennessee.
  • Massachusetts retailer Art Nergaryan saw revenue jump 25 to 35 percent after stocking hemp drinks, but state regulators banned them from wine and liquor stores in May 2024.
  • Congress included a provision in November 2025 legislation to limit total hemp THC to 0.3 milligrams per container, effective November 12, 2026, which effectively bans all THC-infused beverages.
  • The market operates in a legal gray area: federal law prohibits hemp-beverage sales under the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, while state rules vary wildly from embrace to outright ban.
  • Diana Eberlein, chair of the Coalition for Adult Beverage Alternatives, says the patchwork of state rules without federal clarity blocks industry growth and legitimacy.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41% of European vineyards as climate warms

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, has infected vines in Portugal, Spain, Italy, and France since first appearing in Europe on olives in 2013.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face climate risk, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, due to warming and river-valley microclimates.
  • California researchers including Olakunle Olawole, assistant professor at UC Riverside, are sharing decades of outbreak management lessons with Europe on early detection and vector control.
  • Eduardo Moralejo, plant pathologist at Institute for Cross-Disciplinary Physics in Mallorca, warns the bacterium may spread silently before symptoms appear, creating false security until it is established.
  • European countries lack unified communication and reporting requirements; California's experience shows early disclosure and vector management slow spread better than eradication alone once the pathogen is widespread.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent across Europe

  • PIWIs, fungus-resistant hybrid grapes bred by crossing American and European vines, reduce fungicide sprays from 8-15 times yearly to 2-3 times for powdery and downy mildew.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by US researchers in 2019, identifies exact locations of disease-resistance genes, enabling faster breeding and stacking multiple protective layers.
  • Germany leads Europe with 4,000 hectares of PIWI plantings in 2025, ten percent growth on the previous year, accounting for four percent of total German vineyard area.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit versus traditional varieties, chiefly through reduced spraying, fuel, and labor costs.
  • France's ResDur program crosses PIWI varieties with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot to develop disease-resistant hybrids while preserving Bordeaux's distinctive character in experimental plots beginning 2025.

Vineyards deploy netting to cool grape clusters and preserve ripening balance

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and Piedmont are using shade netting to lower cluster-zone temperatures and prevent heat damage to color and acidity during ripening.
  • A 2022 UC Davis trial on Cabernet Sauvignon showed shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded controls, as the pigments degrade above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses hail netting on 90 percent of vineyards, benefiting from filtered light pulses; a new multi-year trial compares 17 percent, 50 percent, and zero shade nets.
  • Petra estate in Tuscany reduced sunburn in Merlot from 41 percent to 3 percent using 90 percent shade nets, but net color matters: white and green nets outperformed black ones.
  • Boroli in Barolo found that netting placement and density require precision; heated fabric touching grapes causes burn, and excessive shade can trap heat and overheat fruit instead.

Exploring the Hunter Valley, Australia’s oldest wine region (1) introduction

  • Hunter Valley became Australia's oldest wine region in 1828 and was the largest in the 19th century; now it has 2,600 hectares with 150 wineries.
  • Hunter Valley Semillon is iconic: picked early at ~11% alcohol, fermented in stainless steel to crisp, bright wine that develops toasty, complex aromas with bottle age.
  • Maurice O'Shea established Mount Pleasant in 1921 and became a revered Australian winemaker, fashioning sought-after red table wines during an era focused on fortifieds.
  • Tyrrell's Vat 47 (1971) claims to be Australia's first commercial Chardonnay; their HVD vineyard, planted 1908, holds some of the world's oldest Chardonnay vines.
  • Semillon dominates plantings at just over 30%, followed by Shiraz; alternative varieties like Albariño, Fiano, and Nero d'Avola show promise in the region.

04The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·12 AUG 2026

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU law limits volatile acidity to 1.2 g/L for red wines and 1.08 g/L for white wines; violations have triggered destruction orders and producer penalties.
  • Sébastien David (Loire) had 2,000+ bottles of Cabernet Franc destroyed in 2019 for exceeding VA limits; a petition against the decision garnered nearly 175,000 signatures.
  • The EU's 1.2 g/L VA limit dates to 1979 and lacks clear scientific justification; vinegar becomes unsafe only above 40 g/L, leaving a large unregulated gap.
  • The US permits 1.4 g/L VA for red wines (1.7 g/L for super-ripe fruit), and the EU's 2025 exceptions list allows up to 2.4 g/L for 400+ appellations including Amarone and Tokaji.
  • Orange wines must comply with white wine VA limits (1.08 g/L) despite their skin-fermentation producing tannin and VA levels similar to reds.

What I Learned at a Conference on Drinking

  • An invitation-only drinking studies conference at Dublin's Museum of Literature Ireland brought together academics from Canada, Poland, UK, and Ireland to study alcohol as cultural and social practice.
  • Researching drinking history is extremely difficult because the practice was so ubiquitous that ordinary people's habits were rarely documented; historical records focus on aberrant behavior or aristocrats.
  • Ireland's 2018 Public Health (Alcohol) Act introduced minimum unit pricing, supermarket barriers, advertising restrictions, and cancer warning labels, the latter pushed back to September 2028 due to international trade pressure from Spain and Italy.
  • Alcohol restrictions historically paralleled colonial justifications used against Irish and Polish populations, raising concerns that public health measures can function as social control.
