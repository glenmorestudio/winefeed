Joel Gott releases electrolyte-spiked wine spritz to court younger drinkers

  • Trinchero Family Wine and Spirits, the third-largest US wine supplier, launched Sauvy B on 15 July through Target, Walmart and Total Wine & More at US$13.99 per four-pack.
  • Sauvy B is a 4.5% ABV canned spritz blending 9% California Sauvignon Blanc with sparkling water and pink Himalayan salt electrolytes, at roughly 100 calories per 355ml can.
  • Founder Joel Gott positioned the product as a recruitment tool for younger, wellness-oriented drinkers rather than existing wine consumers, aiming to embed wine terminology in their everyday vocabulary.
  • The 4.5% ABV electrolyte format now appears standard across functional RTDs, with competitor launches including Stateside Brands' Super Lyte vodka and Svedka's Vodka Water, signalling a market-wide shift.
  • Wine-based RTDs have ceded share to spirit-based canned formats with younger drinkers; electrolytes and low-calorie positioning aim to close that gap alongside planned pineapple and watermelon extensions through January 2027.

Andreola drops Prosecco name to escape mass-market category

  • Stefano Pola, who runs Andreola estate in Valdobbiadene since taking over from founder Nazzareno Pola, removed Prosecco from bottle labels between 2007 and 2018 to distance the wine from the broader, cheaper category.
  • The estate now farms 110 hectares across 250 parcels on steep hillsides in the Rive of Valdobbiadene DOCG, emphasizing territory and handcrafted quality over the Prosecco brand that made it famous.
  • Pola argues the appellation system shift in 2009 made Prosecco a liability: the word now signals volume-driven flatland production rather than hillside quality, forcing him to choose between two stories.
  • Labour scarcity on steep terrain, not climate alone, poses the harder long-term threat; younger workers resist the dawn-to-dusk rhythm dictated by harvest windows and weather.
  • Older producers in the region view Andreola's move as a moral defeat, but Pola frames it as necessary: if the real story is territory and craft, the word Prosecco may no longer serve it.

Costco recalls desert willow plants carrying grape-killing pest

  • Costco sold desert willow plants infested with glassy-winged sharpshooters between 24 June and 3 July; California officials urged customers to inspect purchases immediately.
  • The leafhopper transmits Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease; infected grapevines have no cure and must be destroyed to prevent spread.
  • Pierce's disease blocks water flow in vines, causing leaf discoloration, shriveled grapes, and death; California's $73 billion wine industry faces significant risk from the pest.
  • This is the second Costco-linked sharpshooter discovery in 2026; customers with planted trees should isolate them and contact county agricultural commissioners immediately.
  • Infested plants must be sealed in two bags and discarded in household trash, never compost, to prevent the insect spreading further.

Grape sunburn damage spreads as heat waves reach cooler regions

  • Sunburn occurs when berry surface temperatures exceed 104-109°F under high light, causing chlorophyll and polyphenols to degrade into browning compounds within minutes.
  • Direct sun can heat berry surfaces 22-27°F above ambient air temperature; a 95°F day creates exposed fruit exceeding 122°F on the sun-facing side.
  • Traditional summer defoliation to expose fruit is now a liability in warming regions; late-August heat waves arriving after leaf removal dramatically increase damage risk.
  • Producers are abandoning midseason defoliation, applying kaolin clay reflective films to cool canopy by up to 9°C, and redesigning trellising to shade fruit as prevention.
  • Sunburned grapes produce finished wines with flattened fruit, cooked-green and asphalt notes, astringent tannins, and premature aging that worsen further in bottle.
