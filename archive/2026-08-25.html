Scotch Whisky Association counts 154 distilleries across six regions

  • The Scotch Whisky Association recognizes 154 malt and grain distilleries as of 2026, split across five official regions plus an emerging sixth.
  • The 1784 Wash Act divided Highland and Lowland regions by law, establishing different production rules that shaped distinct whisky styles still recognized today.
  • Speyside, technically a Highland subregion, now hosts over 50 distilleries and is the world's most densely populated whisky region, with distillers choosing to label as Highland or Speyside.
  • Campbeltown shrank from 30 distilleries in the late 1800s to three today: Springbank, Glen Scotia, and Glengyle, yet remains influential for briny, oily expressions.
  • The Islands designation, though informal and technically Highland, emerged from new distilleries on Arran, Jura, Mull, Orkney, Skye, and other isles founded since 2015.

Pierce's Disease Bacteria Detected in Continental European Vineyards

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was first detected on continental European vines in Portugal in 2023 after infecting Mallorca in 2016.
  • Climate modeling shows 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face risk from the disease, up from 21.8 percent in earlier models, as warming winters allow the pathogen to survive.
  • River valley microclimates are especially vulnerable because their topography connects isolated areas and creates conditions where the bacterium can establish itself.
  • California's decades of experience fighting Pierce's disease, including vector management and early detection, offer Europe strategies to slow spread, though eradication becomes impossible once the pathogen is widely established.
  • European growers and regulators lack coordinated reporting and communication systems, risking concealment of infections if vine destruction remains the only mandated response.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays to one-fifth in European vineyards

  • PIWIs, hybrid grapes bred for powdery and downy mildew resistance, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional Vitis vinifera, as demonstrated at Famille Ducourt in Bordeaux.
  • Modern backcrossing has bred PIWIs containing up to 95 percent vinifera genetic material, preserving wine quality while retaining fungal resistance inherited from American vine species.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, used since 2019 under the US VitisGen initiative, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and accelerates breeding by scanning 2,000 markers across 4,000 seedlings in days instead of months.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, a 10 percent increase, while France develops regional ResDur varieties including Bordeaux-signature hybrids crossing Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot.
  • Luxembourg's 2026 study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit reduction, driven by lower chemical, fuel, and labor costs for spraying and reduced soil and water toxicity.
