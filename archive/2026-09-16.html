Seven volcanic regions beyond Etna produce distinctive wines from ancient lava soils

  • Volcanic soils from past eruptions in Soave, Santorini, the Canary Islands, Hungary, Oregon's Willamette Valley, Auvergne, and Lake County create complex terroir shaped by lava composition and layering.
  • Volcanic soils naturally resist pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preserving old vines; they impart signature high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors rather than fruit-forward profiles.
  • Unique growing conditions in these regions, from Santorini's basket-trained vines protecting against wind to Lanzarote's hand-dug holes in black volcanic terrain, shape both viticulture practices and final wine character.

03SevenFifty Daily·17 AUG 2026

Pierce's Disease Detected in Europe; 41% of Vineyards Now at Climate Risk

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was detected in continental European vineyards for the first time in 2023 when one infected vine appeared in Portugal; cases now persist across Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate data found 41.2 percent of European vineyards face climate risk from Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, with river basins and valleys identified as especially vulnerable microclimates.
  • California researchers and growers are sharing decades of experience managing the disease with European counterparts, emphasizing early detection, vector management, and surveillance; California growers cost over $48 million annually managing the disease.
