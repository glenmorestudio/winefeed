Texas High Plains growers struggle as pandemic demand boom turns to buyer caution

  • Texas wineries surged grape purchases during 2020-2023 pandemic sales boom, driving higher prices and aggressive planting; demand then softened and inventories backed up.
  • Growers now face spot-market buying and fewer multi-year contracts, forcing tiered farming: fully contracted blocks get premium care, uncontracted fruit receives minimal investment.
  • A March 2025 freeze cut yields across High Plains and Hill Country, potentially resetting supply-demand balance, but several veteran growers are considering retirement or transition.
  • Younger operators like Connor Wilmeth, vineyard manager at Lahey Vineyards, are pushing eastward distribution and formal contracts with deposit requirements to stabilize relationships.
  • Despite rising critical recognition, the long-distance system linking High Plains growers to Hill Country wineries depends on trust and planning; market uncertainty now threatens both.

English estates launch prestige cuvées priced to match premium Champagne

  • Nyetimber pioneered England's prestige cuvée concept in 2018 with two sparkling wines priced at £150 and £175, matching premium Champagne positioning.
  • Chapel Down's Kit's Coty Coeur de Cuvée, Gusbourne's Fifty One Degrees North, Langham's Perpetual Blanc de Blancs, and Squerryes's Patience 2014 have since joined the super-premium segment, mostly priced £100 and above.
  • Extended lees ageing, typically seven to eight years minimum, and selection from finest vineyard parcels define these releases as markers of quality justifying premium pricing.
  • English estates increasingly adopt Champagne house strategies: named ranges with heritage narratives, small-batch production, vintage specificity, and collaborations with consultants and winemakers.
  • The segment reflects growing recognition of English sparkling wine abroad and domestic positioning as equivalent to, rather than alternative to, Champagne's prestige offerings.

Centre-Loire appellations gain traction as Sancerre prices climb past $40

  • Sancerre Sauvignon Blanc prices have risen to $40, $50 or more, while neighboring Centre-Loire appellations, Pouilly-Fumé, Menetou-Salon, Quincy, Reuilly, Coteaux du Giennois, offer similar quality at $20, $30.

  • Centre-Loire Sauvignon Blancs share Sancerre's minerality and limestone-flint soils but remain less famous internationally, giving nearby regions a residual sales lift from Sancerre's popularity.
  • Winemakers across the region now face earlier harvests due to climate change, with August 20 harvests becoming possible, forcing them to experiment with abandoned acidic varieties to preserve freshness.
  • Pinot Noir from Centre-Loire appellations is gaining attention as quality improves; Matthieu Delaporte, winemaker at Domaine Delaporte in Sancerre, calls the quality-to-price ratio a competitive alternative to Burgundy.

Historic vine-training systems face extinction as labor costs and mechanization advance

  • Alberata vine training, once common across Italy and widespread near Naples, survives on only a handful of hectares today, replaced by mechanizable systems like Guyot and vertical shoot positioning.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo system, developed after volcanic eruptions 1730-1736 buried farmland in lapilli ash, now attracts thousands of tourists annually but faces collapse from extreme labor demands; active vineyard area shrank 19 percent between 2000 and 2026.
  • Alberto González Plasencia, owner of Bodegas Timanfaya in Lanzarote, warns that if hoyos disappear, tourists will stop visiting and wine value will drop; local governments have introduced subsidies to slow decline.
  • Familia Torres maintains traditional pergola training for Rías Baixas Albariño through specialized machinery for ground work; Juan Glaria of Bodegas Rubicon notes that manual harvesting rules in the DO mean all systems require equal labor at picking.
  • Bellussera, a 19th-century pergola-alberata hybrid in Veneto, fell from common use after silk industry and subsistence farming collapsed; it now covers 9 percent of Piave DOC but may survive through climate resilience and wine tourism value.

Brooklyn housing complex installs 450,000 recycled wine corks as playground surfaces

  • Public Housing Community Fund and Cork Collective, an architecture and cork-manufacturing partnership launched in 2024, installed pathways and playgrounds made from 450,000 recycled wine corks at Marlboro Houses in Brooklyn.
  • Cork Collective sourced corks from NYC restaurants and others, shipped them to Wisconsin for grinding and adhesive mixing, then laid them at the site as a $500,000 portion of a $3.2 million open-space renovation.
  • Children at Marlboro Houses submitted design mockups that shaped the final layout, partnering with Center for Justice Innovation, The Urban Conga, and the Marlboro Resident Association.
  • Cork surfaces absorb stormwater, reduce ambient temperature by 20 to 30 percent, and avoid toxic plastic or rubber alternatives; under one percent of 13 billion corks made yearly are recycled globally.
  • NYCHA chief executive Lisa Bova-Hiatt framed the project as proof that resident-led design and cross-sector partnerships can transform public housing amenities.

Light exposure and soil vigor control green pyrazine aromas in wine grapes

  • Methoxypyrazines (MPs) are nitrogen-based compounds causing green, herbal, and pepper aromas in wine, most notably in Cabernet Franc, Sauvignon Blanc, and related varieties.
  • Light exposure is the primary driver of MP concentration in grapes; shoot removal and leaf plucking increase light on clusters and reduce MPs, while shaded canopies increase them.
  • Soil type affects MPs indirectly through vigor: well-drained gravel soils produce lower MP grapes, while water-retaining limestone and clay soils encourage vigorous growth and higher MPs in Cabernet Sauvignon.
  • Rootstock choice influences MP levels by altering vine vigor and canopy density, though whether rootstocks also have direct genetic effects on MPs remains unproven.
  • Temperature appears to correlate with lower pyrazines in cool-climate wines, but the mechanism remains unclear and may be difficult to separate from light effects.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes slash fungicide use by up to 80 percent

  • PIWIs are fungus-resistant hybrid grape varieties bred by crossing American non-vinifera vines with European vinifera, reducing powdery and downy mildew damage without synthetic fungicides.
  • Modern backcrossing techniques now produce PIWIs containing up to 95 percent vinifera genetic material, preserving wine quality while retaining disease resistance from non-vinifera ancestors.
  • Rapid genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019, lets breeders identify and stack multiple resistance genes with precision, cutting fungicide applications from 8-15 sprays to 2-3 per season.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, up 10 percent annually; Bordeaux University and French breeders are developing ResDur varieties to match regional signatures.
  • Luxembourg study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold reduction in environmental impact versus traditional varieties, mainly through lower spraying costs and reduced soil toxicity.

Vineyards deploy netting to cool grape clusters as heat stress intensifies

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and Piedmont are using shade netting in fruit zones to lower cluster temperatures and prevent anthocyanin degradation during heat events, moving beyond traditional bird and hail protection.
  • A 2022 UC Davis study on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more skin anthocyanins than unshaded control during heat stress, because anthocyanins degrade above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • At Petra estate in Tuscany, 90 percent shade nets reduced Merlot sunburn from 41 percent to 3 percent and lowered cluster temperature from 50 to 40 degrees Celsius, but white or green nets performed better than black.
  • Luis Reginato, head of viticulture at Catena Zapata in Mendoza, uses light netting to create brief pulses of filtered sunlight rather than continuous direct exposure, balancing phenolic development against heat stress.
  • Achille Boroli at Boroli estate in Barolo found that netting placement matters critically: nets touching heated fabric can burn grapes, and placement only on sun-facing sides prevents trapping heat around clusters.

03The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·12 AUG 2026

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU law limits volatile acidity in wine to 1.2 g/L for reds and 1.08 g/L for whites, established in 1979 and largely unchanged.
  • Sébastien David (Loire) had 2,000+ bottles destroyed in 2019 for exceeding VA limits; Vizinho Vinhateiro and Pittis faced similar enforcement in 2024-2025.
  • The US allows 1.4 g/L for red wines (1.7 g/L for super-ripe grapes); EU exceptions now permit up to 2.4 g/L for 18 countries' appellations including Amarone, Madeira, and Tokaji.
  • Vinegar requires 40 g/L acidity to form; health risk emerges only above 10% concentration, creating a large safety gap between the 1.2 g/L wine limit and actual hazard.

08Drinks Insider·Felicity Carter·13 AUG 2026

What I Learned at a Conference on Drinking

  • Drinking studies is an interdisciplinary field treating alcohol as cultural and social practice, not merely a drink or health hazard.
  • Researching drinking history is extremely difficult because the practice was so ubiquitous that ordinary people's habits were rarely documented.
  • Ireland's 2018 Public Health (Alcohol) Act introduced minimum unit pricing, supermarket barriers, advertising restrictions, and cancer warning labels, though labels were delayed to September 2028 due to international trade pressure.
  • The Industrial Revolution shifted the meaning of moderation from avoiding disgraceful drunkenness to maintaining sobriety for economic productivity and workplace reliability.
  • Alcohol restrictions have historically been used as a form of social control, British colonizers used stereotypes of Irish drunkenness to justify repression, mirroring contemporary public health arguments.
