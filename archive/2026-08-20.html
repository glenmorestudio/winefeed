Federal cap on hemp THC upends state-by-state beverage markets

  • Massachusetts retailer Art Nergaryan saw THC-infused beverage sales jump 25-35 percent after stocking them, then collapse when state regulators banned their sale in wine stores in May 2024.
  • The THC-infused beverage market grew 135 percent in the 52 weeks to April 2026, reaching $239 million in US retail sales, with Whitney Economics projecting a $9.9-14.9 billion potential market.
  • Congress passed legislation in November 2025 capping hemp-derived THC at 0.4 milligrams per container, effective November 12, 2026, effectively banning most commercial hemp beverage products.
  • States have enacted conflicting rules: Minnesota and Georgia permit hemp beverages while California, Washington, and Vermont restrict or ban them outside licensed cannabis systems.
  • The FDA declared hemp-derived cannabinoid beverages unlawful under federal food law in 2018, yet the industry grew as states set their own rules, leaving retailers navigating legal gray zones.

Ancient vine-training systems vanish as labor costs and mechanization reshape European viticulture

  • Italy's alberata system, once common around Naples and depicted in 18th-century landscapes, survives on only a handful of hectares, replaced by mechanizable methods like Guyot and vertical shoot positioning.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo system, developed after volcanic eruptions buried fertile soil, draws thousands of tourists annually and drives wine sales; active vineyards shrank 19 percent from 2000 to 2026 as labor demands exceeded profitability, prompting local subsidies.
  • Rías Baixas winegrowers maintain traditional pergolas through specialized machinery for upkeep tasks; mandatory hand-harvesting rules mean training method choice no longer affects picking labor, keeping heritage viable.
  • Historical vine-training survival depends on region-specific negotiation between heritage, tourism value, topography, and labor economics; no single approach applies universally across Europe's fragmented viticultural landscape.

Soju surges past shōchū in U.S. market as Asian spirits diverge

  • Soju grew 133 percent between 2019 and 2025 in the U.S., now under 1 percent of total spirits volume, while shōchū grew only 11 percent over the same period.
  • Koryn Ternes, IWSR consulting director for the Americas, attributes soju's acceleration to post-2020 momentum, particularly 2023 to 2025, versus shōchū's slower niche trajectory.
  • Spec's, a Texas retailer, expanded Asian spirits purchases from under $1 million in 2018 to multi-millions today, driven by younger consumers, Korean cultural interest, and soju's fruity flavors and lower ABV.
  • Green bottle soju, mass-produced since a 1965 rice ban forced use of cheaper starches, dominates U.S. exports; Jinro has held world best-selling spirit status for 25 consecutive years.
  • Premium craft soju, led by brands like Tokki Soju founded in Brooklyn in 2016, is emerging as consumers mature; shōchū brands including iichiko and Mizu target bartenders with higher-proof cocktail expressions.

Pierce's Disease detected in Europe; 41% of vineyards now at climate risk

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was first detected on European grapevines in Mallorca, Spain in 2016, then in Portugal in 2023, and now persists across Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate mapping found 41.2 percent of European vineyards are at climate risk of Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in earlier models, with river valley microclimates especially vulnerable.
  • The bacterium kills vines by blocking water transport, causing dehydration within years; warming temperatures remove the historical winter cold that suppressed it, enabling spread into previously safe regions.
  • California's three-decade battle with Pierce's disease offers Europe strategic lessons: early detection, vector management, and research investment can shift endemic outbreaks from eradication to containment.
  • European countries lack unified reporting mandates and communication strategies, risking vineyard concealment; California's self-reporting culture and absence of destruction requirements enabled faster adaptation to living with the disease.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent

  • PIWIs (pilzwiderstandsfähige) are hybrid grape varieties bred to resist powdery and downy mildew by crossing European Vitis vinifera with fungus-resistant American species; modern PIWIs contain up to 95 percent vinifera genetic material.
  • Jérémy Ducourt, technical director at Famille Ducourt in Bordeaux, reduced fungicide sprays from eight to 15 times yearly for traditional varieties to two to three times for PIWIs over a decade of planting.
  • Genetic technology rhAmpSeq, developed under the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, allows researchers to identify disease-resistance genes with precision and scan 2,000 genetic markers across thousands of seedlings in days instead of months.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study comparing two traditional varieties with two PIWIs showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit, chiefly through reduced spraying, fuel, and labor costs plus soil and water protection.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, up 10 percent from the previous year and accounting for four percent of German vineyard area; France is developing ResDur hybrids combining PIWIs with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot.

Vineyards use netting to cool grape clusters as heat damage rises

  • Vineyard netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is now deployed to lower cluster-zone temperatures and prevent heat damage to color and acidity compounds.
  • A 2022 UC Davis study on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded control, as anthocyanin degradation begins above 35 degrees Celsius.
  • At Petra vineyard in Tuscany, Merlot under 90 percent shade nets reached 40-41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees unshaded, cutting sunburn from 41 percent to three percent in one trial.
  • Net color matters: white and green nets perform better than black nets, which absorb and retain heat; nets must be positioned away from fruit to avoid contact burn.
  • Growers in Mendoza, Barolo, and Tuscany are conducting multi-season trials to calibrate shade levels that prevent heat stress without sacrificing the beneficial UV light needed for phenolic development.

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU limits volatile acidity in wine to 1.2 g/L for reds, 1.08 g/L for whites; the US allows 1.4 g/L for reds, up to 1.7 g/L for super-ripe grapes.
  • Sébastien David (Loire) was ordered to destroy 2,000+ bottles of Cabernet Franc in 2019 for exceeding VA limits; a petition against the decision garnered almost 175,000 signatures.
  • EU exceptions now allow up to 2.4 g/L VA in hundreds of appellations from 18 countries including Amarone, Madeira, and Tokaji aszú; Slovenia permits 1.6 g/L for quality reds aged two years in oak.
  • The current 1.2 g/L EU limit originated in 1979 EEC legislation with vague consumer-protection language; vinegar requires 40 g/L acidity, creating a large regulatory gap with no clear health justification.

08Drinks Insider·Felicity Carter·13 AUG 2026

What I Learned at a Conference on Drinking

  • Drinking studies is an interdisciplinary field treating alcohol as cultural and social practice, not merely a drink or health hazard.
  • Historical research on drinking is extremely difficult because the practice was so ubiquitous that ordinary people's habits were rarely documented.
  • Ireland's 2018 Public Health (Alcohol) Act introduced minimum unit pricing, advertising restrictions, and cancer warning labels, but labels were delayed to September 2028 due to international trade pressure.
  • The Industrial Revolution shifted the meaning of moderation from avoiding disgraceful drunkenness to maintaining sobriety and punctuality for economic productivity.
  • Historical alcohol restrictions targeting groups like the Irish and Poles have been used as justifications for colonization and social control, a pattern repeated in contemporary public health measures.
