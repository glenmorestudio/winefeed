Grape sunburn damage spreads as heat waves reach cooler regions

  • Grape sunburn occurs when berry surface temperatures exceed 104-109°F under high light, causing chlorophyll and aromatic compounds to degrade and producing flavors of cooked fruit, asphalt, and rubber.
  • Direct sun can push berry surface temperature 22-27°F above air temperature, meaning a 95°F day creates 122°F conditions on sun-facing fruit.
  • Defoliation timing is critical: leaf removal at veraison increases sunburn risk, while removal at flowering allows berries to build photoprotective compounds.
  • Producers including Tyrrell's in Australia and Chêne Bleu in Provence have abandoned or drastically reduced summer defoliation and now rely on kaolin clay sprays and canopy management to shield fruit.
  • Sunburn damage in finished wine, premature aging, smoky and kerosene notes, becomes more pronounced over time, making prevention essential in warming climates.
