Seven US regions seek AVA status to establish wine identity

  • Seven proposed AVAs from California to New Jersey are moving through Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau approval, which can take several years; they range from 4,495 acres in Lake Hennessey, Napa to 723,760 acres in Deschutes River, Oregon.
  • Annapolis-Sea Ranch in Sonoma will focus on Chardonnay and Pinot Noir shaped by coastal fog filtering and redwood influence, while Lake Hennessey in Napa emphasizes Cabernet Sauvignon with cooler nighttime temperatures from air drainage.
  • Central Oregon's Deschutes River, New Jersey's Hunterdon Hills, and New York's Niagara Lake Plain are experimenting with diverse varietals suited to their climates, from hybrids at higher elevations to cool-climate whites and Pinot Noir.

03SevenFifty Daily·7 SEP 2026

Vineyard idling gains traction as growers weather weak demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs by half.
  • Vineyard idling, or mothballing, pauses production for one to three years without removing vines, allowing growers to defer expenses while keeping land and capital intact during weak grape demand.
  • Viticulturists emphasize severe pruning, minimal irrigation at steady rates, weed control, and selective fungicide spraying as critical to keeping idled vines viable for future return to production.
