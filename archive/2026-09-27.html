Seven US regions seek AVA status to define wine identity and terroir

  • Seven winegrowing regions from California to New Jersey are pursuing American Viticultural Area designations from the US Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, a process that takes several years and creates branded recognition for wines and professionals.
  • Annapolis-Sea Ranch in Sonoma County, spanning 26,637 acres with 651 planted acres, will feature cool-climate Chardonnay and Pinot Noir shaped by coastal fog filtering and redwood-forest character on sandy loam soils.
  • Lake Hennessey in Napa Valley, Deschutes River in central Oregon, Hunterdon Hills in New Jersey, and Niagara Lake Plain in New York are also advancing, each defined by distinctive soil, elevation, and climate patterns that shape their signature grape varieties.

Grape growers idle vineyards to cut costs amid weak market demand

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling cut management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites halving expenses.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary pause in production lasting one to three years, allows producers to preserve assets and wait for market recovery without permanent removal.
  • Proper idling requires sustained irrigation, aggressive pruning, and weed control; vines prepared carelessly risk years of damage when returning to production.

Viña González Bastías, Maule Valley, Chile: exciting naturally made wines from an amazing old vineyard

  • Viña González Bastías in Maule Valley, Chile operates a six-hectare vineyard planted in the early 19th century with vines over 200 years old, run by fifth-generation owner José Luís Bastías and agronomist Daniela Lorenzo.
  • The vineyard uses natural winemaking: traditional wooden Zaranda de Coligüe for destemming, concrete fermentation with no additions, and maturation in amphora or Raulí oak foudres, with minimal sulfites added before bottling.
  • Primary varieties are País, Moscatel Negra, Semillon, and Torontel; periodic flooding from three converging rivers (occurring roughly every 20 years) replenishes vineyard nutrients and occurred in 2023.
