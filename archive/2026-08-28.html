Seven volcanic wine regions rival Etna for mineral complexity and old vines

  • Volcanic soils worldwide, from Santorini to Oregon's Willamette Valley, produce wines marked by high acidity, salinity, and savory flavors rather than fruit-forward profiles.
  • Santorini's Assyrtiko and Hungary's Tokaji Aszú gain depth and mineral tension from volcanic terrain; Santorini vines over 200 years old survive because volcanic regions resist disease.
  • The Canary Islands' phylloxera-free volcanic soils preserved old-vine varieties like Listán Blanco and Listán Negro, sparking a winemaking revival in the 1980s and 1990s.
  • Oregon's Dundee Hills and Eola-Amity Hills volcanic Jory soils yield red-fruit Pinot Noirs with more acidity and grip than marine-sediment counterparts in Willamette Valley.
  • France's Auvergne and California's Lake County, both dormant volcanic zones, now attract natural winemakers seeking terroir suited to organic and low-intervention viticulture.

Pierce's Disease Bacterium Now Threatens 41% of European Vineyards

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines by blocking water transport, has been detected in continental Europe since 2023, with cases now confirmed in Portugal, Spain, Italy, and France.
  • A 2025 study using high-resolution climate modeling shows 41.2 percent of European vineyards face climate risk from Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in previous estimates, with vineyard microclimates in river basins and valleys especially vulnerable.
  • Unlike California, where infected vines are not mandated for destruction, many European regions require eradication, creating risk that growers may conceal infections rather than report them and lose vineyards.
  • Researchers warn that symptom-free spread may create false security in Europe; the bacterium could establish widely before obvious epidemics occur, shifting management from eradication to containment once endemic.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide spraying by 80 percent

  • PIWIs are hybrid grapes bred from non-Vitis vinifera and European vinifera stock to resist powdery and downy mildew, reducing fungicide sprays from 8-15 times yearly to 2-3 times.
  • Modern genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019 under the US VitisGen initiative, maps 2,000 genetic markers per vine, accelerating identification of resistance genes and reducing breeding time from months to days.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, a 10 percent annual increase; France is developing RegDur varieties that retain regional character, with Bordeaux experimentally crossing them with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit versus traditional varieties, chiefly through reduced spraying costs and lower soil and water toxicity.
  • PIWI wines remain 71 percent direct-to-consumer sales in Germany; retail expansion hinges on supermarket adoption and competitive pricing that translates vineyard gains into shelf appeal.
