China's newest Master of Wine says trade fairs need strategy, not just presence

  • Liu Weiran, China's newest Master of Wine, researched exhibitor preferences at Chinese wine trade fairs for his MW thesis, finding different exhibitor types prioritize cost, location, buyer access and promotion differently.
  • China's largest drinks fair, the Tangjiuhui in Chengdu, has seen exhibitor incomes fall while stand costs remain high; fairs now function as showcases rather than venues for immediate bulk orders.
  • Liu advises foreign wineries entering China to attend fairs despite lower returns, but combine attendance with social media promotion, livestreams, dinners and branded items to capture valuable buyer traffic.

02Vino Joy News·29 SEP 2026

New Zealand wine exports surge in Asia as U.S. sales decline

  • New Zealand shipped 306.2 million liters of wine in the year through June 2026, up 6.04%, but total export revenue rose only 0.48% to NZ$2.108 billion.
  • Mainland China imports jumped 71.91% by volume to 7.344 million liters, though value rose just 24.27% to NZ$69.841 million, signaling lower per-liter prices.
  • U.S. exports fell 7.31% by volume and 5.39% by value despite remaining New Zealand's largest market; Britain and Australia also saw volumes outpace earnings growth.

New Zealand wine gains tariff cuts in India from October 2026

  • New Zealand, India Free Trade Agreement takes effect October 20, 2026, reducing India's 150% wine import tariff to 25% or 50% based on bottle value; wines under US$5 per 750ml remain at full duty.
  • Tariff reductions phase in over a decade with larger cuts favoring premium bottles, but NZ exporters still face state-level taxes, excise duties and alcohol bans that vary by region.
  • Wine consumption concentrates in Mumbai, Delhi and Bengaluru; Maharashtra imposes 40% VAT on wine while other states levy between 50% and 65% excise duty depending on wholesale price.

01SevenFifty Daily·1 OCT 2026

Researchers map how grapevines defend against smoke, opening treatment paths

  • When smoke-exposed vines bind volatile phenols to sugar molecules via glycosylation, the compounds become sensory-inactive; but enzymatic activity during fermentation can unbind them and cause smoke taint to reappear unpredictably.
  • Scientists at Adelaide University found Chardonnay activates 15 genes across five enzyme families under smoke stress, while Shiraz activates only five genes across two families, suggesting variety-specific defensive responses could guide treatment selection.

How Can You Train to Recognise Wine Faults?

  • Sietze Wijma founded The Art of Tasting in 2021 to teach wine professionals and enthusiasts to recognize wine faults using synthesized flavor compounds mimicking real-world defects.
  • Wijma launched an Online Tasting Course in wine faults after studying sensory science and working three years at a UK laboratory producing aroma compounds for training.

Moldova is an important wine country, and increasingly it’s making some really good wines

  • Moldova has 109,700 hectares of vineyards across latitudes 45-49°N; approximately 80% planted with international varieties and 20% with Caucasian and local varieties including Saperavi, Rară Neagră, and Fetească.

04Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 OCT 2026

Red and White and Roero All Over

  • Roero, north of Alba across the Tanaro River in Piemonte, produces white Arneis and red Nebbiolo on sandstone soil, with 1,000 planted hectares of white versus 360 of red.
  • Roero Nebbiolo is approachable with limber tannins and bright acidity; Arneis is peachy, floral, and saline; U.S. prices range $25, $45 for reds and rarely exceed $25 for whites.
  • Some Roero producers label reds as Langhe Nebbiolo or Nebbiolo d'Alba to access laxer production rules and more recognizable trade names rather than the Roero D.O.C.G. designation.
