Moldova emerges as high-density wine nation with rare indigenous varieties

  • Moldova has 114,000 hectares of vineyards covering 7% of its agricultural land, the highest vineyard density globally, with wine accounting for 3% of GDP and 8% of exports.
  • The Fetească family of indigenous white and red varieties, Fetească Albă, Fetească Regála, and Fetească Neagră, offers no equivalents elsewhere and is being explored in new oak and traditional method sparkling wines.
  • Rară Neagră, possibly cultivated since 3000 BC and now centered in Stefan Vodă, produces light reds with sour cherry and wild blackberry notes that rival Pinot Noir at lower cost.
  • Viorica, a 1969 crossing developed for frost resistance, is a distinctly floral white wine pairing particularly well with Asian cuisine and representing Moldova's winemaking innovation.
  • Two state-owned limestone-quarry cellars, Cricova with 70km of galleries and Mileștii Mici with over 200km holding 1.5 million bottles, provide ideal maturation conditions and are major wine tourism sites.

Costco recalls desert willow plants carrying grape-killing pest eggs

  • Costco sold desert willow plants infected with glassy-winged sharpshooter eggs at US stores between 24 June and 3 July 2026.
  • The leafhopper spreads Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease, a fatal infection with no cure that destroys grapevines.

  • This is the second Costco recall this year involving the pest; California's $73 billion wine industry faces major vulnerability to Pierce's disease.

Grape sunburn spreads north as heat waves intensify; growers shift canopy tactics

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response when berry surface temperatures exceed 104-109°F under high light, causing cell death, chlorophyll and polyphenol breakdown, and floral/citrus aroma loss.
  • Direct sun can push a berry 22-27°F above air temperature; a 95°F day makes exposed fruit exceed 122°F on the sun-facing side.
  • Producers in Provence and Hunter Valley have reversed decades of leaf-removal practice, now leaving canopy cover through summer and limiting defoliation to early flowering or vigorous varieties only.
  • Kaolin clay spray cools canopy by up to 9°C, used by Tyrrell's in Australia since 2014; sorting sunburned fruit at harvest is the final defense when prevention fails.
  • Climate change is pushing heat waves into regions without historical sunburn risk, making shading and timing of canopy management critical to preserve acid, color, and aromatic compounds.

Even Heat-Tolerant Mourvèdre Struggles as Southern France Burns

  • Mourvèdre, bred for heat and drought in southern France's Bandol region, now faces temperatures and dry spells so severe that even old vines suffer and die.
  • Jean-François Ott at Domaines Ott and other Bandol producers are experimenting with canopy management, higher pruning, and ground cover to shade grapes and retain soil moisture.
  • A three-year drought in Jumilla from 2022 to 2024 forced growers to consider irrigation, threatening the dry-farming tradition that defines the region's terroir.
  • Bandol's cooler sites once reserved for rosé are now being considered for red wine as climate shifts, risking a reduction in the region's rosé production.
  • In Jumilla, the vineyard surface area has halved in 20 years, with lower-altitude sites abandoned; only higher-altitude parcels at 600-800 meters retain the conditions Mourvèdre needs.
