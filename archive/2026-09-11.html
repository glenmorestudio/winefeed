Grape growers idle vineyards to cut costs as market demand weakens

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, or mothballing production for one to three years, allows growers to preserve capital and avoid removal of vines when grape sales weaken but future demand is uncertain.
  • Successful idling requires careful vineyard maintenance: adequate year-round irrigation, aggressive pruning, weed control, and disease monitoring to ensure vines can resume production within two years.

Seven volcanic wine regions rival Mount Etna for mineral complexity

  • Volcanic soils from ancient and active eruptions create complex, layered terroirs that produce high-acidity wines with savory, mineral, and saline flavors distinct from other regions.
  • Santorini, Soave, the Canary Islands, Tokaji, Willamette Valley, Auvergne, and Lake County all show how volcanic geology shapes grape varieties, vine training methods, and final wine character.

  • Xylella fastidiosa bacterium, which causes Pierce's disease in grapevines, was detected in continental Europe in 2023 when a single infected vine appeared in Portugal; cases now persist in Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.

06Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wines undergo second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, then are disgorged and dosed; they develop nutty, complex flavors from bottle conditioning and aging.
  • Tank method (Charmat/Martinotti) uses pressurized tanks for second fermentation, producing cleaner, fresher sparkling wines with less lees contact than traditional method.
  • Skin-contact wines macerate white grapes with skins and seeds during fermentation, creating color and tannin; the tradition originated in the Caucasus and has resurged in the past two decades.
