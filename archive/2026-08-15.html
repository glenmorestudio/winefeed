Texas High Plains growers struggle as wine sales soften after pandemic surge

  • Texas wineries saw explosive sales growth from 2020 to early 2023, then demand softened and inventories backed up, leaving High Plains growers with unsold fruit.
  • Growers now receive vastly different farm investment depending on contract status: fully contracted blocks get premium care; uncontracted fruit gets minimal maintenance to preserve vine health.
  • Multi-year contracts have become rare; wineries increasingly wait until harvest to buy cheaper fruit on the spot market instead of committing upfront to High Plains growers.
  • A March 2025 freeze reduced yields significantly, which some growers view as a painful market reset that could rebalance supply and demand.
  • Younger vineyard operators like Connor Wilmeth, 33, are building East Coast distribution networks and formal grape contracts with deposits to stabilize sales beyond local Hill Country wineries.

EU quality labels guarantee sparkling wine origin and production standards

  • The EU Quality Label system uses PDO, PGI, and Organic certifications to guarantee sparkling wines meet strict standards for origin, production method, safety, and sustainability across member states.
  • PDO-labeled sparkling wines like Champagne, Prosecco, Cava, and Austrian Kamptal must be grown, produced, and aged entirely within their designated region using traditional methods.
  • PGI-labeled sparkling wines require only one production stage within a broader region, allowing greater flexibility in grape varieties and winemaking techniques than PDO wines.
  • EU Organic certification cannot appear on wine bottles sold in the US due to lack of mutual recognition with USDA, though winemakers may indicate organic grape production separately.
  • These labels help consumers identify authentic regional sparkling wines and support small producers maintaining traditional techniques and biodiversity across European wine regions.

  • Soju grew 133 percent in US spirits volume between 2019 and 2025 to less than 1 percent of total market, while shōchū rose only 11 percent to a tiny fraction of 1 percent, according to IWSR consulting director Koryn Ternes.

Ancient vine-training systems vanish as labor costs and mechanization reshape European vineyards

  • Lanzarote's hoyo pits, created after volcanic eruptions buried fertile soil, draw thousands of tourists annually and drive the island's tourism economy, yet labor demands threaten survival.

Light exposure in vineyard controls green-aroma compound levels in wine grapes

  • Methoxypyrazines (MPs), nitrogenous compounds causing bell pepper and grass aromas, accumulate early in grape development then sharply decline at veraison, possibly to deter animals before seeds mature.
  • Light exposure is the primary driver of MP concentration: shoot removal and leaf thinning that increase cluster sunlight reduce MPs, a finding established by Dominique Roujou de Boubee at University of Bordeaux in 1995.
  • Rootstock choice affects MP levels indirectly through vigor, which determines canopy density and shade; eight Cabernet Sauvignon rootstocks showed significant MP variation in grapes and stems, says David Jeffery, associate professor at University of Adelaide.
  • Soil type influences MPs via water retention: well-drained gravel soils produce lower MP Cabernet Sauvignon than clay-silt soils in Bordeaux, while irrigation timing can mimic low-water effects in managed vineyards.
  • Temperature appears correlated with lower MP intensity in cool-climate Cabernet Francs and Loire wines, yet the mechanism remains unclear, as vineyard heat alone cannot chemically degrade MPs.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide use by up to 80 percent

  • PIWI grapes, hybrids bred for powdery and downy mildew resistance, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional Vitis vinifera varieties.
  • Modern genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, lets breeders identify exact locations of disease-resistance genes instead of general regions, accelerating development.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental benefit versus traditional varieties, mainly through reduced spraying costs and lower soil and water toxicity.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, four percent of national vineyard area and 10 percent growth year-on-year; France is developing regional ResDur varieties to retain Bordeaux signature.
  • Direct-to-consumer sales dominate PIWI market share at 71 percent in Germany, with retail expanding as supermarket chains begin stocking them, though consumer awareness remains low.

Vineyard netting emerges as climate tool to cool ripening fruit zones

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and Piedmont are using shade netting to lower cluster-zone temperatures and protect anthocyanins during heat events.
  • A 2022 UC Davis trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters retained 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded controls when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius.
  • Petra estate in Tuscany reduced Merlot sunburn from 41 percent in unshaded blocks to three percent under 90 percent shade netting in 2025 field trials.
  • Net color and placement matter: white or green nets perform better than black, and fabric must stay clear of clusters or risk becoming a heat source itself.
  • The strategy requires balance; excessive shade risks trapping heat, limiting airflow, and altering phenolic development, making calibrated exposure the real challenge.

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU law limits volatile acidity in wine to 1.2 g/L for reds, 1.08 g/L for whites; violations have led to bottle destruction orders and widespread petition support.
  • US allows 1.4 g/L for red wines, up to 1.7 g/L for super-ripe grape wines; EU's 2025 exceptions list permits up to 2.4 g/L for 18 countries' appellations including Amarone, Madeira, and Tokaji.
  • The 1.2 g/L limit originated in a 1979 EEC regulation with vague consumer-protection justification; vinegar requires 40 g/L minimum, leaving a large unexplained gap.
  • Orange wines must conform to white wine's lower 1.08 g/L limit despite having tannin and VA levels similar to red wines.

What I Learned at a Conference on Drinking

  • Drinking studies is an interdisciplinary field treating alcohol as cultural and social practice, not merely a drink or health hazard.
  • Historical research on drinking is extremely difficult because the practice was so ubiquitous that people rarely documented ordinary drinking habits.
  • Ireland's 2018 Public Health (Alcohol) Act introduced minimum unit pricing, advertising restrictions, and cancer warning labels, though labels were delayed to September 2028 due to international trade pressure.
  • The meaning of moderation shifted in the 18th century from avoiding disgraceful drunkenness to self-regulation required by Industrial Revolution factories needing reliable, punctual workers.
  • Alcohol restrictions have historically been used as tools of social control and repression, including against Irish and Polish populations, masquerading as health concerns.
