Texas High Plains growers struggle as wineries cut grape purchases

  • Texas wineries saw explosive sales from 2020 to early 2023, then demand softened and inventories backed up, leaving High Plains growers with unsold fruit.
  • Growers including Katy Jane Seaton at Farmhouse Vineyards now tier vineyard care by contract status, reserving premium farming for committed buyers only.
  • Multi-year contracts have become rare; some wineries wait until harvest to buy cheaper fruit on the spot market, forcing growers to plan without certainty.
  • Producers like Connor Wilmeth at Lahey Vineyards and Kim McPherson of McPherson Cellars are seeking East Coast buyers to reduce dependence on local Hill Country wineries.
  • Veteran growers including Jet Wilmeth and Neal Newsom are considering retirement or reduced operations, threatening continuity of benchmark vineyard sites.

  • Soju sales rose 133 percent between 2019 and 2025 in the U.S., now under one percent of total spirits volume, while shōchū grew only 11 percent over the same period, reflecting soju's acceleration post-2020.

  • Shōchū brands iichiko and Mizu target bartenders as key ambassadors, emphasizing higher-proof options around 40 percent ABV for cocktail applications to compete with tequila and mezcal drinkers.

RNDC files Chapter 11 bankruptcy after shedding majority of US markets

  • Republic National Distributing Company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Texas federal court yesterday after a year of contraction, seeking court oversight to sell remaining operations and wind down.
  • RNDC lists liabilities between $1 billion and $10 billion against assets of $500 million to $1 billion, with over 100,000 creditors and $300 million in top unsecured claims led by Proximo Spirits' $93.92 million trade debt.
  • The distributor shed California operations last June, then sold 11 markets to Reyes Beverage Group and stakes to Columbia Distributing, Martignetti Companies, and BreakThru Beverage Group while claiming to preserve 5,000 jobs.
  • Roughly 1,450 workers remain employed; RNDC sought immediate court permission to continue payroll and benefits to prevent staff exodus during reorganization.
  • Small suppliers holding unsecured claims face high risk of total loss, as they lack leverage to demand prepayment and cannot easily route products through competing distributors.

Ancient vine-training systems decline as labor costs and mechanization pressure historic landscapes

  • Italy's alberata system, shown in 1793 paintings intertwining vines with trees across Naples, survives on only a handful of hectares today, replaced by mechanizable methods like Guyot.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo pits, carved after 1730-1736 volcanic eruptions to reach soil beneath lapilli ash, drew thousands of tourists and supported the island's economy until labor demands forced 19 percent vineyard abandonment between 2000 and 2026.
  • Alberto González Plasencia of Bodegas Timanfaya and Juan Glaria of Bodegas Rubicon warn that without subsidies, Lanzarote's hoyos will disappear and tourist visits, and wine values, will follow.
  • Familia Torres maintains Rías Baixas pergola training for Albariño on six hectares at Pazo Torre Panelas using specialized machinery, viable because the region's DO bans mechanized harvesting across all systems.
  • Veneto's 19th-century bellussera pergola-alberata hybrid, which once supported silk production and livestock, covers nine percent of Piave DOC but may survive through climate-change adaptation and wine tourism, if regions balance heritage with economics.

Disease-resistant hybrids cut fungicide sprays 80%, gain European vineyard ground

  • PIWIs (fungus-resistant hybrid grapes) reduce fungicide sprays from 8-15 times yearly to 2-3, cutting chemical, fuel, and labor costs by up to 80 percent.
  • German breeders pioneered modern PIWIs in the late 20th century; today's varieties contain up to 95 percent Vitis vinifera genetic material while retaining mildew resistance from American species.
  • Genetic technology rhAmpSeq, adopted globally since 2019, identifies disease-resistance genes with precision and cuts breeding timescales from months to days via marker-assisted selection of seedlings.
  • Germany leads adoption with 4,000 hectares of PIWI plantings in 2025 (4 percent of vineyard area, 10 percent annual growth); France develops ResDur hybrids bred to maintain regional signature varieties like Cabernet Franc.
  • Luxembourg 2026 study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental gains; retail expansion accelerating as supermarket chains stock PIWIs alongside traditional wines.

Vineyards use netting to cool fruit zones as heat stress intensifies

  • Vineyard netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is now deployed to lower cluster temperatures and preserve color, acidity, and phenolics during heat events.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius during heat spikes.
  • At Petra in Tuscany, Marco Simonit's 2025 trials showed Merlot clusters under nets measured 40-41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees in unshaded controls, reducing sunburn from 41 percent to three percent.
  • Luis Reginato at Catena Zapata in Mendoza found nets intercept just 15 percent of radiation in high-altitude vineyards, allowing filtered light while reducing water stress on vines.
  • Net color and placement matter critically: white or green nets perform better than black, and nets must stay away from clusters to avoid heat damage from contact with the material itself.

Grape sunburn spreads north as heat waves force vineyard practice shifts

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response to excess light and heat that degrades chlorophyll, polyphenols, and aromatic compounds, producing flavors of asphalt, rubber, and leather in the finished wine.
  • Berry surface temperatures can reach 122°F on a 95°F day; Chardonnay shows damage within five minutes at 104-109°F under high light, while cell death occurs at 126°F.
  • Producers in warm regions including Provence and Napa now avoid mid-summer leaf removal, limit defoliation to east-facing sides only, and skip it entirely for white varieties due to heat-wave risk.
  • Tyrrell's in Australia's Hunter Valley has used kaolin clay spray since 2014 to cool canopy by up to nine degrees Celsius, while Matthiasson Wines redesigns trellis and orientation to shade fruit.
  • Climate change is pushing sunburn damage into regions that never faced it before, forcing growers to prioritize canopy cover and sorting over traditional sun-exposure practices.
