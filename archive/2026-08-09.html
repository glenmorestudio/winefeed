Ancient vine-training systems fade as labor costs and mechanization reshape European wine regions

  • Italy's alberata system, which trained vines on poplar and elm trees across Naples until the late 20th century, survives on only a handful of hectares, replaced by mechanizable methods like Guyot.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo pit system, developed after volcanic eruptions in 1730-1736, shrank from 2,326 hectares in 2000 to 1,889 by 2026 despite boosting tourism, due to extreme labor demands and low planting density.
  • Alberto González Plasencia of Bodegas Timanfaya warns that losing hoyos would cut tourist visits and reduce wine value, prompting local subsidies to preserve the system.
  • Spain's Rías Baixas region sustains pergola training for Albariño because mandatory hand-harvesting rules make its labor cost no higher than modern trellises, and Familia Torres uses adapted machinery for ground work.
  • Veneto's bellussera hybrid system, which once supported silk production and livestock fodder, fell from prominence but may revive as climate change makes it more resilient and wine tourism creates new economic value.

Vineyards test netting to cool grape clusters as heat stress intensifies

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and Piedmont are deploying shade netting over fruit zones to lower cluster temperatures and protect color compounds during heat events.
  • A 2022 UC Davis trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius, where color degradation begins.
  • At Petra in Tuscany, Marco Simonit's 2025 trials reduced Merlot sunburn from 41 percent to three percent under 90 percent shade, with cluster temperatures dropping from 50 to 40 degrees Celsius.
  • Luis Reginato at Catena Zapata in Mendoza emphasizes filtered light exposure over full sun; the estate uses light netting to stress vines less in hot, dry conditions without sacrificing useful radiation.
  • Net color and placement matter: white and green nets outperform black nets, and nets must stay clear of clusters to avoid trapping heat against fruit; darker nets do not always cool better than open ones.
0313 JUL 2026

Grape sunburn spreads beyond hot climates as heat waves intensify

  • Sunburn in grapes is a stress response when berry surface temperatures exceed 104-109°F under high light, causing chlorophyll and aromatic compounds to degrade and producing flavors of asphalt, leather, and creosote.
  • Direct sun can heat exposed berries 22-27°F above ambient air temperature; in red varieties, heat damages anthocyanins more severely than light does, according to UC Davis research.
  • Defoliation timing is critical: removing leaves at veraison creates more sunburn risk than removing them at flowering, when berries build photoprotective compounds; many warm-region producers now skip summer leaf removal entirely.
  • Producers including Tyrrell's in Hunter Valley and Chêne Bleu in Ventoux are adopting kaolin clay particle film and reducing canopy exposure, with some achieving up to 9°C cooler fruit zones in sprayed blocks.
  • Practices long standard in moderate climates, such as midsummer defoliation and east-side fruit exposure, are being retimed or eliminated as earlier and more intense heat waves reach regions unprepared for sunburn damage.

Global alcohol deaths fall 26% since 2010, analysis shows

  • Alcohol-attributable deaths worldwide fell 26% between 2010 and 2024, according to analysis by the International Alliance for Responsible Drinking using Global Burden of Disease data.
  • Disability-adjusted life years linked to alcohol fell 23% over the same period, while high alcohol use exposure declined 10%.
  • The improvement likely reflects combined effects of changing drinking patterns, healthcare improvements, public health initiatives and broader social development, not a single intervention.
  • Progress varies significantly by country and region, masking disparities in how different populations have reduced alcohol-related harm.
  • The 26% reduction exceeds the UN's 2010 target of 20% reduction in harmful alcohol use by 2030, though sustained effort is required.
