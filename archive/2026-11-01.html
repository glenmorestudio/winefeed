Enartis explains matching oak alternatives to specific winemaking objectives

  • Oak alternatives differ in extraction rate, toast level and sensory impact, so product choice depends on the style goal.
  • Oak chips added during fermentation do little for colour stability because oxygen is scarce then.
  • Colour stability benefits most when oak is combined with controlled oxygen exposure, such as micro-oxygenation, during maturation.

06IVES Open Science·22 OCT 2026

MODIS satellite data validated for mapping vineyard water use in Northeast Italy

  • Researchers at the University of Padova used MODIS satellite data to estimate evapotranspiration in a 25-hectare Italian vineyard.
  • Estimates were calibrated on two years of data and independently validated against four years of eddy-covariance measurements.
  • Results matched in-situ observations, suggesting MODIS could monitor vineyard water consumption at the regional scale.

07IVES Open Science·22 OCT 2026

Rootstocks and irrigation shape Grenache water use in NE Spain trial

  • Three-year-old Grenache vines on 110-R, 41-B and 161-49C rootstocks were tested irrigated and non-irrigated in NE Spain.
  • Non-irrigated vines had higher water use efficiency, and 110-R and 41-B outperformed 161-49C.
  • Stomatal conductance differences from irrigation persisted through the season, suggesting hormonal signals may matter more than hydraulic ones.

  • A 2007 trial in Vinhos Verdes compared ethephon, Regalis and manual bunch thinning on Loureiro and Borraçal vines.

10IVES Open Science·22 OCT 2026

Vine balance defined as the match between vegetative growth and fruiting

  • Vine balance means a vine's vegetative growth matches its reproductive growth, ripening the crop while setting next year's buds.
  • Indirect measures such as yield to pruning weight ratio are commonly used, but they do not capture seasonal growth dynamics.
  • A dynamic carbohydrate simulation model can show seasonal patterns of growth demand against canopy carbon supply.
