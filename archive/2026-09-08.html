Vineyard operators idle acres to cut costs as grape market stalls

  • A 2025 study of 19 northern Willamette Valley vineyards found idling reduced annual management costs by an average of 38 percent per acre, with some sites cutting costs in half.
  • Vineyard idling, a temporary production pause lasting one to three years, allows growers to preserve assets and capital during weak demand rather than remove vines permanently.
  • Key idling protocols include aggressive pruning, minimal irrigation year-round, and light fungicide spraying to maintain vine health for future production without exhausting financial reserves.

Seven volcanic wine regions beyond Mount Etna showcase terroir shaped by ancient eruptions

  • Volcanic soils from past eruptions in Soave, Santorini, the Canary Islands, Hungary, Oregon's Willamette Valley, Auvergne, and Lake County create mineral-driven wines with high acidity and savory notes.
  • Volcanic terrain presents farming challenges: Santorini vines train low in basket shapes to resist wind; Lanzarote requires hand-dug individual planting holes called hoyos protected by stone walls.
  • Volcanic soils resist pests and disease, enabling organic farming and preserving old vine varieties; phylloxera never reached the Canary Islands, allowing indigenous grapes like Listán Blanco to survive.

05Maker's Table·Meg Maker·1 SEP 2026

M is for Method

  • Traditional method sparkling wine undergoes second fermentation in bottle with sugar and yeast, creating complexity through bottle conditioning; variants include méthode traditionnelle (France), metodo classico (Italy), and Cava (Spain).
  • Ancestral method, the older sparkling technique, completes fermentation in bottle without disgorgement, remaining fully dry and cloudy with retained lees; favored by natural winemakers.
  • Tank method (Charmat/Martinotti) uses pressurized tanks for secondary fermentation, producing clean, direct sparkling wines with minimal lees contact.
