US wine industry lacks coordinated marketing apparatus to counter negative coverage

  • Gino Colangelo, founder of New York PR agency Colangelo & Partners, says US wine lacks the collective marketing infrastructure that dairy, almonds and cranberries sectors maintain.
  • Come Over October, a pro-bono campaign co-founded by Colangelo and wine critic Karen McNeil, counters rising anti-alcohol media narratives by positioning wine as agricultural and social.
  • Colangelo warns that inconsistent funding and company support for wine advocacy campaigns, coming in 'fits and starts', prevents sustained market-building unlike regional rivals Chianti Classico and Prosecco DOC Consortium.
  • Wine regions that sustain incremental marketing campaigns build lasting market share, while those launching big programs then disappearing for years lose traction, Colangelo says.
  • Current US wine downturn is cyclical but its depth depends on continued industry investment in marketing and advocacy, Colangelo argues, amid competition from THC beverages and weight-loss drugs.

  • Endeavour Group, Woolworths' wine subsidiary, is selling physical assets of Chapel Hill Estate in McLaren Vale and four other Australian wineries to cut costs by AU$300 million.
  • Endeavour bought Chapel Hill in 2019 for an undisclosed sum, folding it into new division Paragon Wine Estates; the company will retain the brand but offload its 13.69 hectares of vineyard and winery infrastructure.

Australian wine exports hit 22-year low amid global consumption decline

  • Wine Australia reported exports fell 7% in value to A$2.3bn and 6% in volume to 598 million litres in the year to June 2026, the lowest volume since 2004.
  • Global wine consumption has fallen to its lowest level since 1961, driven by cost-of-living pressures, changing consumer habits, and competition from alternative alcoholic beverages.
  • Australia's three largest markets all declined: mainland China dropped 15% to A$756m, while the UK and US posted their lowest volumes in 25 years.
  • Canada emerged as a bright spot, with exports rising 20% in value to A$188m, partly due to market-share gains from reduced US wine availability during trade disputes.
  • Several Asian markets outside China recorded gains, with Thailand achieving record export values and Singapore becoming Australia's largest Asian market outside mainland China.

Vineyards deploy shade netting to cool fruit zones as heat stress intensifies

  • Growers in California, Argentina, Italy, and France are using shade netting to lower cluster temperatures and protect color compounds during heat stress, a shift from netting's traditional role as bird and hail defence.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon showed shaded clusters had 27 percent more skin anthocyanins than unshaded controls when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius, the threshold for anthocyanin degradation.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses light netting that blocks 15 percent of radiation; trials compare nets blocking 17, 50, and zero percent shade to measure effects on vine stress and phenolic compounds.
  • At Petra in Tuscany, Merlot clusters under 90 percent shade nets measured 40 to 41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees in unshaded fruit, reducing sunburn from 41 percent to three percent in one trial.
  • Net colour and placement matter: white or green nets absorb less heat than black; nets must stay away from clusters to avoid heat damage, and excessive shade can trap heat and compromise wine quality.

Grape sunburn spreads north as heat waves reshape canopy management

  • Chris Tanghe, MS and director of education for GuildSomm, identified sunburned grapes by sensory markers: flattened fruit, rubber, asphalt, and creosote notes that mask wine complexity.
  • Joanna Gambetta, Ph.D., wine scientist at University of Newcastle Australia, found berry surface temperatures can reach 22 to 27 degrees Fahrenheit above air temperature; Chardonnay shows damage within five minutes at 104 to 109°F under high light.
  • Sunburn degrades chlorophyll, polyphenols, carotenoids, and aromatic terpenes while reducing tartaric acid; in red varieties heat damages anthocyanins more than light does, per UC Davis 2009 research.
  • Producers including Chêne Bleu in Ventoux, Tyrrell's in Hunter Valley, and Matthiasson Wines in Napa have abandoned or retimed midsummer defoliation, adopted kaolin clay film, and redesigned canopy to shield fruit as heat waves intensify.
  • Steve Matthiasson, winemaker at Matthiasson Wines, emphasizes sorting sunburned fruit at harvest is critical: poor grapes will degrade an entire batch regardless of winemaking skill.
