Scotland's 154 distilleries span five official regions plus emerging Islands cluster

  • The Scotch Whisky Association counts 154 malt and grain distilleries as of 2026 across five SWA-protected regions: Highland, Lowland, Speyside, Islay, and Campbeltown.
  • Speyside, in the northeastern Highlands along the River Spey, holds over 50 distilleries and is the world's most densely populated whisky region, known for sweet, fruit-forward styles.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41 Percent of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • Xylella fastidiosa, a bacterium that causes Pierce's disease, has infected grapevines in Portugal, Spain, and Italy; a 2025 climate study shows 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face risk, up from 21.8 percent in prior models.
  • The pathogen spreads via glassy-winged sharpshooter insects and kills vines by blocking water transport; warming winters mean previously safe regions, especially river valleys and microclimates, are becoming vulnerable.
  • California growers lost over $48 million annually to Pierce's disease in the late 1990s and early 2000s; European regulators are now learning from that experience to improve surveillance, early detection, and vector management.
  • European Union member states are conducting annual surveys and eradication in isolated cases, but lack coordinated communication strategies; growers fear mandatory vine destruction, which threatens historic vineyards.
  • Olakunle Olawole, assistant professor at University of California Riverside, notes that once the bacterium becomes established, management shifts from eradication to slowing spread, as California has learned over decades.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide spraying by 80 percent

  • PIWIs (fungus-resistant hybrid grapes) reduce fungicide sprays from 8-15 times yearly to 2-3 times, cutting chemical, fuel, and labor costs while lowering environmental toxicity.
  • Modern genetic testing (rhAmpSeq) lets breeders pinpoint disease-resistance genes with precision and stack multiple resistance layers, accelerating development of new varieties.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares in 2025, up 10 percent annually, while Bordeaux and Luxembourg are developing region-specific resistant hybrids retaining local grape signatures.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental benefit compared to traditional varieties managing the same mildew pressure.
  • PIWIs now contain up to 95 percent Vitis vinifera genetic material and behave like traditional vines in the vineyard, requiring no significant change to existing practices.

Vineyards deploy netting to cool grape clusters amid heat stress

  • Vineyard netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is being repositioned to lower cluster temperatures and preserve color and acidity during heat events.
  • A 2022 UC Davis trial on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius, the degradation threshold.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza uses light netting to filter direct sunlight into brief pulses rather than continuous exposure, reducing vine stress in high-altitude desert conditions without depleting beneficial UV radiation.
  • Petra estate in Tuscany tested nets on Merlot and reduced sunburn from 41 percent to three percent under 90 percent shade, with white and green nets outperforming black nets that absorb excess heat.
  • Boroli in Barolo found that open-weave nets on sun-facing sides lowered peak cluster temperatures from 49.2 to 43 degrees Celsius, but net placement and density matter; contact with grapes risks burn damage.
