Federal THC beverage cap wipes out state-legal hemp drink sales

  • Massachusetts retailers selling hemp-derived THC drinks faced a ban in May 2024 when state regulators prohibited their sale in wine and liquor stores, erasing revenue overnight.
  • Art Nergaryan, owner of Art's Specialties in Massachusetts, saw his business jump 25 to 35 percent after adding THC beverages, then collapse when the ban took effect and distributors removed stock.
  • The US THC-infused beverage market grew 135 percent in the 52 weeks to April 2026, reaching $239 million, but faces a federal cap of 0.4 milligrams per container effective November 2026.
  • States have adopted conflicting rules: Minnesota and Georgia permit hemp beverages, while California, Washington, and Vermont restrict them outside licensed cannabis systems, creating an inconsistent national market.
  • The 2018 Farm Bill legalized hemp but not hemp-beverage products; Congress's November 2025 legislation closes loopholes by capping total THC concentration at 0.3 percent dry weight, effectively banning most current drinks.

English estates launch prestige cuvées at Champagne prices

  • Nyetimber pioneered England's prestige cuvée market in 2018, releasing white and rosé 1086 wines at £150, £175, matching Champagne prices, from the 2009 vintage onwards.
  • Chapel Down, Gusbourne, Langham and Squerryes have since launched their own super-premium bottlings, priced £109, £395, each with extended lees ageing and limited production runs.
  • These cuvées draw from specific vineyard parcels and vintage selections, with ageing baselines of seven to eight years on lees, mirroring prestige Champagne production models.
  • English prestige cuvées typically carry heritage narratives: Squerryes invokes a 17th-century wool merchant; Gusbourne named its range after vineyard latitude; Langham uses a solera-style perpetual blend inspired by grower Champagne.
  • The movement reflects growing international recognition of English sparkling wine quality, enabling estates to command premium positioning previously reserved for Champagne houses.

  • Eleven US sommeliers were asked which red wines carry reputations and prices that outpace their quality in the glass.

Ancient vine-training systems face extinction as labor costs and mechanization advance

  • Italy's alberata system, once widespread around Naples, survives on only a handful of hectares today, replaced by mechanizable methods like Guyot and vertical shoot positioning.
  • Lanzarote's hoyo pits, carved after 1730-1736 volcanic eruptions, draw thousands of tourists annually and support the island's economy, but vineyard area fell 19 percent from 2000 to 2026 due to labor demands.
  • Alberto González Plasencia, owner of Bodegas Timanfaya on Lanzarote, warns that losing hoyos would cost the island tourism revenue and reduce wine value despite local subsidies.
  • Familia Torres maintains six hectares of traditional pergolas for Rías Baixas Albariño at Pazo Torre Panelas using specialized machinery, proving adaptation can sustain heritage systems where DO regulations support hand harvest.
  • Bellussera, a 19th-century pergola-alberata hybrid near Venice, covers only nine percent of Piave DOC today but may recover as climate change favors its resilience and wine tourism creates new economic value.

Pierce's Disease Threatens 41% of European Vineyards as Climate Warms

  • Xylella fastidiosa, a bacterium causing Pierce's disease, has been detected in grapevines in Portugal (2023), Spain's Balearic Islands (2016), and regulated zones across continental Europe, with potential economic damage estimated at €5.5 billion.
  • A 2025 high-resolution climate study found 41.2 percent of European vineyards now face climate risk of Pierce's disease, up from 21.8 percent in earlier models, as warming winters allow the pathogen to survive in previously protected regions.
  • Vineyard microclimates in river basins and valleys are especially vulnerable because they connect isolated areas that historically stayed too cold for the bacterium to persist.
  • California's decades of fighting Pierce's disease show that early detection and integrated vector management are critical; once the pathogen becomes established, eradication shifts to slowing spread and minimizing damage.
  • European countries lack consistent communication protocols between regional bodies and growers, risking concealment of infections and hindering coordinated surveillance efforts that California now recognizes as essential.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide sprays by 80 percent in vineyards

  • PIWIs, hybrid grapes bred for fungal resistance, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties like Cabernet Franc.
  • Modern genetic testing technology rhAmpSeq, pioneered by the US VitisGen initiative in 2019, pinpoints disease-resistance genes with precision, accelerating PIWI development globally.
  • Germany leads PIWI adoption with 4,000 hectares planted in 2025, up 10 percent year-on-year and representing four percent of German vineyard area.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study found PIWIs deliver twofold economic savings and up to threefold environmental benefit, chiefly through reduced chemical, fuel, and labor costs in spraying.
  • New French ResDur hybrids crossing PIWI stock with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot are entering trial plots in Bordeaux to maintain regional flavor while building mildew immunity.

Vineyards use netting to lower cluster temperatures and preserve color

  • Vineyard netting, traditionally used for hail and bird protection, is being deployed to reduce solar radiation and lower cluster-zone temperatures during heat events.
  • UC Davis research on Cabernet Sauvignon found shaded clusters retained 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded controls when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius, the threshold for color degradation.
  • At Petra in Tuscany, Merlot clusters under 90 percent shade nets measured 40-41 degrees Celsius versus 50 degrees in unshaded controls, cutting sunburn from 41 percent to three percent in one trial.
  • White and green nets perform better than black nets; placement matters, nets must stay away from fruit to avoid transferring heat, and one-sided shading avoids trapping warm air as an oven effect.
  • Catena Zapata in Mendoza and Boroli in Barolo are studying net shade levels to balance UV exposure needed for phenolic development against heat stress, calibrating light pulses rather than full exposure.

04The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·12 AUG 2026

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU law limits volatile acidity in wine to 1.2 g/L for reds, 1.08 g/L for whites; violations can result in bottle destruction orders.
  • Sébastien David (Loire) had 2,000+ bottles of Cabernet Franc destroyed in 2019 for exceeding VA limits; a petition against the decision garnered almost 175,000 signatures.
  • The US permits 1.4 g/L VA for red wines, up to 1.7 g/L for super-ripe grapes; the EU's 2025 exceptions list allows up to 2.4 g/L for hundreds of appellations including Amarone, Madeira, and Tokaji.
  • The current 1.2 g/L limit was established by the European Economic Community in 1979 with vague consumer-protection justification; vinegar requires 40 g/L acidity to form.
  • Orange wines must conform to the lower white wine VA limit of 1.08 g/L despite their tannin and VA levels typically resembling reds.

  • Drinking studies, an interdisciplinary field treating alcohol as cultural and social practice, is severely hampered by lack of historical records on ordinary people's drinking habits.
  • The 18th-century gin craze may have subsided due to changing fashion rather than regulations or harvests, suggesting drinking trends are often poorly documented.
  • Industrial Revolution factories shifted the meaning of moderation from avoiding disgrace to maintaining sobriety for productivity and punctuality.
