Federal ban on hemp THC drinks takes effect November 2026, upending state markets

  • Congress passed legislation in November 2025 capping THC at 0.4 milligrams per container, effective November 12, 2026, banning most hemp-derived THC beverages nationally.
  • Hemp THC-infused drink sales hit $239 million in the 52 weeks prior to April 2026, with the potential market valued at $9.9 to $14.9 billion.
  • Art Nergaryan's Massachusetts wine and cheese shops saw revenue jump 25 to 35 percent after stocking THC drinks, but state regulators banned their sale in wine stores in May 2024.
  • State regulations remain inconsistent: Minnesota and Georgia permit hemp beverages, while California, Washington and Vermont restrict or ban them outside cannabis-licensed retail.
  • Diana Eberlein, chair of the Coalition for Adult Beverage Alternatives, says the patchwork of state rules without federal clarity has blocked industry growth and legitimacy.

Albanian wine industry emerges after 30 years of isolation

  • Albania produced 3.03 million liters of wine in 2022 and exported $560,000 in 2024, mostly to Europe, with sporadic US sales in Texas and Michigan.
  • Grape production jumped over 250 percent between 2000 and 2016 as producers trained in Italy, Greece, France, and Germany returned with new winemaking techniques.
  • The country has no official geographically designated regions but grows distinct styles across northern, central, and southern zones shaped by elevation, soil, and climate.
  • Indigenous grapes including Kallmet, Shesh, Debina, Pulës, and Vlosh are gaining prominence alongside international varieties as producers adopt small-scale and minimal-intervention methods.
  • Tourism and consumer education campaigns frame Albanian wines as Mediterranean or continental expressions, positioning the nation for broader global recognition over the next 10-20 years.

Pierce's Disease Threat Expands Across Europe as Climate Warms

  • Pierce's disease, caused by the bacterium Xylella fastidiosa, devastated California's wine industry from the late 1990s onward, costing growers over $48 million annually and killing large vineyard blocks.
  • The disease was detected in European grapevines on Spain's Mallorca island in 2016 and in continental Europe in Portugal in 2023, with cases now confirmed in Spain, Italy, Portugal, and France.
  • A 2025 climate study found 41.2 percent of European vineyards face climate risk, up from 21.8 percent in previous models, with riverside and valley microclimates especially vulnerable as winters warm.
  • California's decades of experience show early detection, vector management, and rapid diagnostics can slow spread once the disease becomes established, though eradication requires swift action.
  • European growers and regulators lack unified communication strategies and mandatory reporting systems, risking delayed detection and potential concealment of infections if vine destruction remains the only solution.

How light exposure and soil control green pepper aromas in wine grapes

  • Methoxypyrazines, nitrogen-based compounds causing bell pepper and grassiness, accumulate early in berry development then decline sharply at veraison in varieties like Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc.
  • Light exposure on grape clusters is the primary factor controlling pyrazine concentration; shoot removal and leaf plucking reduce shade and lower these aromas, research from Dominique Roujou de Boubee's 1995 University of Bordeaux study showed.
  • Soil type affects pyrazines indirectly through vine vigor: gravel soils with low water retention produce lower pyrazine grapes, while limestone and clay-silt soils encourage vegetative growth and higher concentrations in Cabernet Sauvignon.
  • Rootstock choice influences pyrazine levels in both grapes and stems by altering canopy vigor, though Dr David Jeffery of University of Adelaide notes genetic effects of rootstock remain unresolved.
  • Temperature appears to correlate with lower pyrazine intensity, but the mechanism remains unclear; winemaker Duncan Meyers of Arnot-Roberts in Napa observes cooler vintages and cooler sites consistently show higher pyrazine expression.

Disease-resistant hybrid grapes cut fungicide spraying by up to 80 percent

  • PIWI grapes, bred to resist powdery and downy mildew, require two to three fungicide sprays per season versus eight to 15 for traditional varieties like Cabernet Franc.
  • Genetic technology rhAmpSeq, deployed since 2019 under the US VitisGen initiative, allows breeders to identify disease-resistance genes with precision and stack multiple protections into new hybrids.
  • Modern PIWIs now contain up to 95 percent Vitis vinifera genetic material through backcrossing, retaining traditional wine quality while inheriting American vine fungal resistance.
  • A 2026 Luxembourg study showed PIWIs delivered twofold economic savings and threefold environmental benefit versus traditional varieties, with gains in soil ecology and reduced water toxicity.
  • Germany leads adoption with 4,000 hectares of PIWI vines in 2025; Bordeaux and Swiss breeders are developing regionally-adapted hybrids including ResDur crosses with Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot.

Vineyards deploy shade netting to cool clusters and preserve phenolics in heat

  • UC Davis found that shaded Cabernet clusters at Oakville had 27 percent more anthocyanins than unshaded fruit when temperatures exceeded 35 degrees Celsius, the threshold for pigment degradation.
  • Luis Reginato, viticulture head at Catena Zapata in Mendoza, uses light netting and canopy management to deliver filtered sunlight in brief pulses rather than sustained direct exposure to prevent heat stress.
  • Marco Simonit's 2025 trials at Petra in Tuscany showed that shade netting reduced Merlot cluster temperature from 50 to 40-41 degrees Celsius and cut sunburn incidence from 41 percent to three percent.
  • White and green nets outperformed black nets in trials, absorbing less heat; nets must be positioned away from clusters to prevent the heated fabric itself from burning fruit.
  • Achille Boroli at Boroli estate in Barolo uses one-sided netting on south-facing slopes to cool clusters without trapping heat, reducing hours above 35 degrees Celsius from 210 to far fewer annually.

04The Morning Claret·Simon J. Woolf·12 AUG 2026

Who's Afraid of Volatile Wines?

  • EU law limits volatile acidity to 1.2 g/L in red wines; Loire vigneron Sébastien David was ordered to destroy 2,000+ bottles of Cabernet Franc exceeding this limit in 2019.
  • The EU's current 1.2 g/L volatile acidity limit originated in 1979 and lacks clear scientific justification; vinegar requires 40 g/L to form, and 10% concentration is needed for health concern.
  • The US permits 1.4 g/L volatile acidity in red wines, rising to 1.7 g/L for super-ripe grapes; EU exceptions now allow up to 2.4 g/L in 18 countries' hundreds of appellations.
  • Orange wines must meet the white wine volatile acidity limit of 1.08 g/L despite having tannin and VA levels similar to reds, creating regulatory inconsistency.

What I Learned at a Conference on Drinking

  • Drinking studies treats alcohol as cultural and social practice rather than a drink or health hazard, an interdisciplinary field that includes historians, anthropologists, sociologists, and other academics.
  • Researching drinking history is extremely difficult because drinking was so ubiquitous that ordinary people's habits were rarely documented; historical records focus on aberrant behavior or aristocrats.
  • Ireland's 2018 Public Health (Alcohol) Act introduced minimum unit pricing, supermarket barriers, advertising restrictions, and cancer warning labels; labels were pushed back to September 2028 from May 2026.
  • Spain and Italy challenged Ireland's cancer warning labels on alcohol via EU and WTO, not Big Alcohol as claimed; they opposed labels on valued cultural products.
  • The Industrial Revolution shifted the meaning of moderation from avoiding disgraceful drunkenness to maintaining worker sobriety, reliability and punctuality tied to economic productivity.
